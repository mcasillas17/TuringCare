# NOTES - Canine development & life stages as training inputs

Scope: what age/stage/history should change in a deterministic, force-free training program generator. Row numbers (#n) refer to LINKS.md. Confidence tags: high / med / low. "Convention" = widely used expert practice with no direct trial evidence; encode as defaults the owner or editor can tune, never as clinical claims.

## 1. Key findings (with citations)

**Developmental periods.** The standard scheme (Scott & Fuller 1965, #8; restated in #11): neonatal 0-2 wk, transition 2-3 wk, socialization 3-12 wk, juvenile 12 wk-6 mo, pubertal/adolescent ~7-24 mo. Definitions vary (neonatal 0-12 d vs 3-16 d; transition ~15-21 d: #18). The original experiments were few, small, lab-based, and few breeds; modern controlled evidence is thin because withholding socialization is unethical (#11).

**Socialization period.** Freedman 1961 (#9, via #11): human contact introduced after ~5 wk produced increasing withdrawal; after ~14 wk normal human relationships were not established in kennel-reared pups. Guide-dog work (Pfaffenberger & Scott 1959, #10): kennel pups homed after 12 wk did worse. Modern correlational support: fearful dogs had fewer socialization experiences and poorer maternal care (#13 Tiira); poor puppyhood socialization, low activity/training, urban living associated with fear of strangers/dogs (#12 Puurunen); maternal investment in first 5 wk predicted guide-dog success (#15); breeder-side enrichment 0-6 wk improved later behavior (#14); homing at <=1-2 mo associated with more fear/attention-seeking vs >=4 mo (#17), and litter separation at 30-40 d with more problems (#16). The 2022 systematic review (#11) finds 29 studies, mostly retrospective questionnaires, and explicitly says the minimum effective dose and breed-specific timing are unknown. Howell 2015 (#6) concludes socialization should start with the breeder and continue lifelong.

**Vaccination vs. socialization.** AVSAB (#1): socialization before full vaccination should be standard of care; classes from 7-8 wk with >=1 vaccine set >=7 d earlier and deworming; avoid dog parks / unsanitized high-traffic places; classes indoor, cleanable. Stepita 2013 (#2): vaccinated puppies in classes were not at higher parvo risk (small attender group, observational). AAHA 2019 (#3) concurs (per summary).

**Fear periods (contested).** Classic teaching: a fear period ~8-10 wk and another in adolescence. Lazzaroni 2019 (#21): in 12 dogs / 13 wolves tested at 6,10,14,18,22,26 wk, dog fear of novel objects fell steadily; no evidence of rising fear after 8 wk. Breed-dependent onset has been reported (secondary). An adolescent "second fear period" has essentially anecdotal support only (guide-dog authors call adolescence "the most poorly studied" period; #22). What IS supported: single frightening events in adolescence associate with later fear/aggression (being threatened by a dog -> dog-directed fear and stranger aggression; frightened by a person -> stranger fear and lower training success; #22, correlational).

**Adolescence.** Asher 2020 (#5): at ~8 mo, guide-dog candidates were less likely to respond to "sit" from their carer (OR 2.14) but not from a stranger; carer-rated trainability dipped from 5 to 8 mo and recovered by 12 mo; separation-related behavior 36% higher at 8 mo; insecure attachment -> earlier puberty. Authors: do not punish or emotionally withdraw. Generation Pup (#24, n=1,219): owner-perceived undesirable behaviors 29.9% (6 mo) -> 34.0% (9) -> 42.1% (12) -> 35.1% (15) -> 32.0% (18); barking, lead pulling, recall problems peak ~12 mo; mouthing and toileting decline steadily with age (mouthing people 1.6% -> 0.3%; toileting 2.3% -> 0.7%). Foyer 2016 (#22): between 6-12 mo owner-directed aggression, separation problems declined, German Shepherd stranger-directed aggression rose.

**House training.** No peer-reviewed study I found gives a "reliably house trained by X weeks" timeline. What exists: toileting complaints decline from 6 to 18 mo (#24); >=4 accidents/week at <=16 wk predicts more separation-related behavior at 6 mo (#26, may reflect general anxiety, correlational); 57% of puppy classes in the UK cohort gave no house-training advice (#25); house soiling is among the most common first-weeks complaints for adopted dogs (secondary report: Vitulova #31). Ex-breeding dogs show more house-soiling (#34). So timelines must be presented as owner-management expectations (frequency-of-opportunity, schedule), not developmental facts.

**Bite inhibition.** I found no peer-reviewed primary study on development of bite inhibition. Claims ("learned from littermates, most intense 8-16 wk", "yelp-and-pause") trace to Scott & Fuller and trainer convention (Donaldson, Dunbar). Epidemiology: mouthing people declines from 1.6% to 0.3% between 6 and 18 mo (#24). Treat as convention.

**Puppy classes.** Observational benefits: better trainability/obedience, stranger response (#50), family-dog aggression/non-social fear (#49), retention in home (#51), fewer aggression/compulsion/destruction/barking odds when training <=6 mo (#23). No difference found between starting at 1-3, 4, or 5-6 mo (#23). Evidence is "mixed" overall (#6, #11). Class quality is variable: 41.7% of attended classes did not allow puppy play, 57% lacked house-training advice (#25); owners most often skip classes because they want to train themselves (57.4%) (#25).

**Rehomed / shelter / adult adopted dogs.** Bohland 2023 (#28; 99 dogs, 7/30/90/180 d): vs day 7, stranger-directed aggression, chasing, and training difficulty rose at all later points; excitability and touch sensitivity rose at 90/180 d; separation-related behaviors and attention-seeking fell at 180 d; owner satisfaction high (93.7% good/excellent); returns 7.1%. The paper does not mention the 3-3-3 rule; it uses the idea of a "honeymoon" period of dynamic change. NZ adolescent/adult adoptees (#30): 70% had >=1 problem; poor manners 46%, destruction 30%, high energy 28%, aggression to people/dogs 19% each; of 25 followed dogs, 52% unchanged, 20% resolved, 20% worse. Vitulova (#31, via secondary summary): ~72% of adopters reported problems in week 1. Returns: ~22% within 3 mo in one cohort, 58.6% of returns for behavior (#29, via search summary); owners relinquishing under-recognize problems (#32). Shelter stress: dogs rest less at night especially first 2 nights, cortisol elevated through day 12, large individual variation, no clear first-night-specific effect (#33). Ex-breeding dogs: more fear of people/dogs/novelty, more house-soiling, less aggression (#34). Street dogs: adopters report fear initially in most dogs, with confidence growing and strong bonds (#35, summary level); no study tests socialization after the sensitive period.

**Senior dogs / CCD.** CCDR (#39): 13 items from 27, accuracy 98.9%, PPV 77.8%, NPV 99.3% on validation (abstract). CADES (#40): 17 items, 4 domains (spatial orientation, social interaction, sleep-wake, house soiling), tracks progression (about 42% normal->mild and 24% mild->moderate in 6 mo). Prevalence is heavily age dependent: Dog Aging Project risk +52% per additional year after age 10 (#41); other surveys 14-35% of dogs >8 yr, ~28% at 11-12 (#45, #4); AAHA quotes 14-22.5% >8 yr (#4). Hopper 2024 (#42): dual vision+hearing impairment associated with cognitive impairment (OR 1.8-2.0) in >=8 yr dogs; causation unknown. Milan 2020 (#48): sensory capacity should be checked before cognitive testing. Activity: lower activity associates with higher CCD risk (#43, observational). Treatments: diet and enrichment partially effective in lab beagles; one pet-dog trial of diet+training in healthy dogs null (#47); selegiline is the only labeled drug (#4). Owners often miss early signs (#4, #46).

**Exercise / growth.** The "5 minutes per month of age" rule has no primary-study basis that I could find (only vet blogs, some stating no evidence). Krontveit 2012 (#36): in four large breeds, stairs at 0-3 mo increased hip-dysplasia risk; off-leash exercise on soft, uneven ground was protective. Neutering <1 yr associated with joint disorders in large/giant breeds with strong breed dependence, none in <20 kg mixed breeds (#37, #38). Skeletal maturity by size: small/toy ~9-12 mo, medium ~12-15, large/giant 18-24 (consumer sources; no primary radiographic source retrieved - low confidence).

## 2. Strong vs. weak vs. contested

**Strong (multiple converging lines):** early socialization/habituation helps later sociability and reduces fear (consensus, though dose unknown); owner-directed adolescent obedience dip around 8 mo is real (#5); punishment/aversive methods associated with more problem behaviors (#1, #26, #27); behavior problems peak ~12 mo and many mouthing/toileting problems fade with age (#24); senior risk of CCD rises steeply with age and co-occurs with sensory loss (#41, #42); newly adopted dogs' behavior keeps changing for months (#28, #30).

**Weak / low-quality:** exact socialization window boundaries (3-12 vs 3-14 vs 3-16 wk, breed-dependent); class benefit size; house-training age norms; bite-inhibition developmental timeline; "start training at 7 wk" (no benefit of 1-3 vs 5-6 mo start in #23); skeletal-maturity ages by size; ENS/Bio Sensor (Battaglia, no independent trials; #18, #20).

**Myths / contested (flag in UI copy):**
- "Fear period at 8-10 wk and 6-14 mo" - not supported as a fixed window (#21); adolescent fear period anecdotal. Use "if the dog shows new fear, back off" rather than calendar-triggered avoidance. Do not tell owners to avoid all new exposure for weeks.
- "The 3-3-3 rule" (3 days decompress, 3 weeks learn routine, 3 months feel at home) - rescue-community heuristic, no peer-reviewed validation (#53). Bohland (#28) found behaviors still changing at 30-180 d. Present as a pacing metaphor only. Days-long adjustment is supported by shelter physiology (#33).
- "Puppy must wait until fully vaccinated" - contradicted by AVSAB/AAHA/Stepita given precautions (#1, #2, #3).
- "Socialization window closes abruptly at 12-14 wk" - overstated; adults can still improve (#5/#28/#30 show change), but fearful dogs are harder; evidence for post-window socialization in street dogs is absent.
- "5 minutes per month exercise" - not evidence-based; keep as soft cap with a flag (see rules).
- "Spay/neuter by 6 mo for everyone" vs "wait until maturity" - breed/size dependent, contested (#37, #38); outside a training engine's remit: link to vet.
- "Dominance"/alpha rolling - not supported (AVSAB positive-reinforcement statement #1).
- Puppy socialization = flooding at puppy parties: AVSAB says avoid overstimulation (excessive fear, withdrawal, avoidance).

## 3. ENGINE RULES

Notation: `dog.age_weeks`, `dog.age_months`, `dog.size` in {toy_small, medium, large, giant}, `history` in {from_breeder_home, shelter_known, shelter_unknown, street, breeding_stock, owner_surrender, unknown}.

### 3.2 Puppy socialization checklist and targets (deterministic)
1. IF age_weeks <= 16 THEN generate a weekly "positive exposure" checklist spanning categories: people (ages, genders, hats/uniforms/beards, mobility aids, kids with supervision), vaccinated/health-known friendly dogs, surfaces, sounds, household noises, car travel, handling (paws, ears, mouth, collar, grooming, vet exam), alone time, novel objects, environments. Target: ~3-5 new items/day, ~20-30 distinct items/week (target values: low confidence - convention; #1 only says "as many as can be achieved safely"; #11 says dose unknown). Confidence on structure: high; on numbers: low.
2. Every exposure carries an ABORT/BACK-OFF rule: IF puppy shows freezing, hiding, trembling, refusal to eat treats, or avoidance THEN increase distance, end session, log as "sub-threshold needed" (#1: avoid overstimulation). high.
3. Vaccination gating: IF puppy has >=1 vaccine set >=7 days ago AND dewormed THEN class/home-visit exposures allowed to vetted classes (clean surfaces, health-checked dogs); ELSE restrict to owner's home, carried outings (car, pavement-free), vaccinated adult dogs known to owner. Dog parks, pet-store floors, unvetted high-traffic dog areas = excluded until vet clears. (#1, #2, #3) high. Always show: "ask your vet about local parvo risk".
4. IF puppy is >16 wk without prior socialization THEN keep the same checklist but slower (lower exposure intensity, more distance) and flag "less certain outcome"; do not announce "window closed". (#11, #5) med.
5. Quality > quantity: IF 3 consecutive bad exposures logged (fear signs) THEN reduce category intensity and suggest professional consult. med.
6. Class advice: IF age_weeks >= 7 and vet clearance THEN recommend a puppy class with: positive reinforcement only, small groups, health checks, supervised play with matching size, house-training advice included, no flooding (#1, #25). Cap age/size mismatch. med.
7. Breeder-side (for breeder-facing content only): handling from birth; avoid ENS marketing claims (#18). low.

### 3.3 Fear / sensitive-phase handling
- RULE: IF user logs a sudden new fear (any age 8 wk - 18 mo) THEN pause new-exposure items in that category 3-7 days (convention), continue easy successes, check pain/health, then re-introduce at lower intensity. Do not attribute to a "fear period" in copy; say "common in growing dogs; handle gently". (#21, #22) med.
- RULE: IF a frightening incident occurs (attacked by dog; frightened by person) THEN raise priority of "recovery" plan: reduce triggers, choose controlled positive counter-experiences, avoid dog-park/greeting-heavy outings for 2-4 weeks (convention), recommend professional if reactivity persists >2 weeks. (#22) med.

### 3.4 Adolescence rules (6-18 months)
- On entering adolescent band, show expectation card: "Obedience to you may dip around 8 mo (recovers ~12 mo); this is normal" and enable `regression_tolerance`: do not mark skill failures as owner failure; temporarily increase reinforcement rate, reduce criteria, switch to higher-value rewards. (#5) high.
- NEVER schedule or suggest punishment/aversive tools; add "stay engaged" prompt if owner reports frustration/disengagement; flag rehoming risk ("peak relinquishment ~8 mo", #5) -> show support/consult. high.
- Recall: use long line until 90% success across 3 distractions (convention); (#24: recall issues peak 9-15 mo). med.
- Prevent unrehearsed hazards: management > training for adolescence (fences, long line). convention.
- Increase exercise/enrichment targets adaptively (Tiira #13: low daily exercise associated with noise sensitivity and separation anxiety; correlational). low-med.
- Neutering questions -> out-of-scope; show vet-decision note; large/giant caution (#37, #38). low.
- Breed-risk flag: IF German Shepherd type AND age 6-12 mo AND owner reports rising stranger wariness THEN suggest early professional help and controlled stranger exposure (#22). low-med.
- Sexual maturity/heat/mounting: expect 6-14 mo (female first heat); warn that behavior changes during heat (convention).

### 3.5 Adopted / rehomed / adult dogs (history-driven)
- Decompression window: for ALL newly adopted dogs, first ~3-14 days (default 7) = "settling plan": minimal demands, predictable routine, quiet area, no new introductions/visitors, no formal training except name/marker, optional chew/lick/sniff enrichment. Source: shelter stress physiology (#33: cortisol and poor rest persist ~2 days to >12 days, big individual variation) and general consensus. Do NOT say "3 days is enough": use variable `decompress_days = 7` default, 14 for fearful/ex-breeding/street/unknown (med; days are convention, rationale from #33).
- Present 3-3-3 only as optional narrative, labelled "rule of thumb, not scientific" (#53, #28). Show that behavior may CHANGE at 30-90 d: expect rises in training difficulty, touch sensitivity, excitability, stranger-directed wariness (#28). high for "expect change".
- Milestone check-ins at days 7, 30, 90, 180 mirroring #28; prompt questions from C-BARQ-like items (stranger-directed aggression, chasing, touch sensitivity, separation, training difficulty). If any "worse" at 90 d, show "common, escalate to pro if bite risk".
- First-month prevalence expectation copy: majority of adopters report some problem (63-72%; #30, #31) -> set expectation, reduce returns (#29: expectations matter). med.
- Unknown history: treat as `fear_unknown`: skip trigger-heavy exposure, go slow on handling/introductions, prefer management, run a 2-week observation log before goal planning. (#34, #35, #30) med.
- Ex-breeding/puppy-mill/hoarding: priority = environmental novelty (stairs, doors, leash, outdoors, floors), human contact at dog's pace, house-training from scratch (#34), no forcing; recommend veterinary behaviorist if fear persists. med.
- Street/free-ranging adopted: leash/house/harness introduction slowly; expect initial fear that eases (#35); flag escape risk and neophobia; recommend secure fencing/double leash; low-med.
- Adolescent/adult adoptees: start with house routine + toileting from scratch (assume untrained), basic manners (46% poor manners; #30), then recall on long line. 52% of problems persist at follow-up (#30): schedule long support, not 2-week program. med.
- Aggression history or bite on record: do not generate DIY desensitization; route to DACVB/CAAB/vet (see section 4). high.
- Behavior in the shelter does not predict home behavior reliably: don't over-trust shelter notes (inference; shelter vs home differences #33); low.

### 3.6 Seniors and CCD
- IF dog in last 25% of expected lifespan (#4) THEN: (a) reduce session length (3-8 min), increase frequency, lower criteria; (b) prioritize scent games, "find it", gentle novelty, puzzle feeders (AAHA sensory enrichment, olfaction retained - secondary), mat/settle, cooperative care, stairs/hill avoidance if mobility issues; (c) keep sessions at times of day when dog is alert; (d) maintain routine/predictability. med (rationale), low (numbers).
- Sensory-first screening: IF age >= 8 THEN ask owner about hearing, vision, mobility; add switch to visual/touch cues (hand signals, vibration, light) when hearing loss suspected, and scent/auditory cues when vision loss; use consistent lighting and non-slip floors. (#42: dual sensory impairment OR 1.8-2.0; #48) med.
- CCD screening: IF age >= 8 AND owner reports >=1 of disorientation, sleep-wake changes, social changes, house soiling, activity changes THEN show CADES/CCDR-style 4-domain screener (DISHAA: disorientation, interactions, sleep-wake, house soiling, activity, anxiety - clinician framework; CCDR 13 items #39; CADES 17 items #40) and recommend vet visit to rule out pain, sensory, endocrine, and other disease BEFORE training (#4). high.
- IF CCD suspected/confirmed THEN program mode = "supportive": ritual, short low-pressure sessions, enrichment, house-soiling management (more frequent outings, no punishment), night routine support; no new complex skills; vet for meds/diet (#4, #47). med.
- House soiling in a senior = medical first; do not generate "house training refresh" without health clearance. high (#40, #4 clinical logic).
- New fear/noise phobia/anxiety in a senior -> vet first (pain, sensory loss, CCD). high.
- Exercise: seniors need regular low-impact activity (#43 association). Suggest multiple short walks, sniffing; modify by mobility status. low-med.
- Don't remove training for "old dog can't learn": older dogs can learn but may be slower, esp. spatial/memory tasks (#44); keep success-rate high. med.

### 3.7 Exercise and growth safety rules
- Replace the "5 min/month" rule with a conservative non-prescriptive guidance table: low-impact, self-paced, short bouts, surface-aware; no forced running/jumping repetition; avoid repetitive jumping/agility jumps until growth plates close (~12 mo small/medium, 18+ mo large/giant: low confidence on ages); avoid stairs in first 3 months for large/giant breeds (#36); allow free off-leash play on soft uneven ground (#36). med for stairs/soft-ground, low for plate ages.
- Cap structured "exercise-like" training (fetch, recall sprints) to short bouts; stop on fatigue or lameness; any limping -> vet. convention.
- Daily activity/enrichment floor for adolescents/adults is associated with fewer noise/separation problems (#13); present as "activity matters" not causal. low-med.

### 3.8 House training (owner-management rules)
- Default schedule (convention): take out on waking, after meals, after play/sleep, every 1-2 h for 8-12 wk, extend gradually; reward within 3 s of finishing; supervise or confine when unsupervised; clean with enzymatic cleaner. No peer-reviewed developmental timeline exists.
- Expected-progress benchmark for UI: "by 16 wk most owners still have some accidents; >=4 accidents/week at 16 wk is a flag to review schedule, and consider anxiety/medical" (#26: association with later SRB; interpretation low). Frequent accidents after previously reliable = vet (UTI, etc.) and stress review. med.
- Adult adoptees: assume untrained for 2-4 wk (house soiling is the most common new-adopter complaint; #31) and treat house-training as management first. med.
- Never punish accidents (aversive training associated with SRB; #26, #27). high.

### 3.9 Bite inhibition / mouthing
- Age 8-16 wk: provide chew items, redirect to toys, time-out (calmly stop play 10-30 s) when mouth pressure hurts; teach "gentle" via reinforcement of soft mouthing; avoid physical corrections. Evidence: convention (no primary study); mouthing declines with age (#24). Confidence low-med.
- IF puppy <8 wk separated/lacks littermates (singleton, early-weaned) THEN raise priority of bite-inhibition and social-play practice with well-socialized dogs (#16/#17 association; low).
- IF mouthing increases to bites with skin puncture or growling/stiffening during handling/resource guarding THEN referral flag (section 4).
- Teething 3-6 mo: expect heavy chewing; provide chilled chews (convention).

### 3.10 Session & plan parameters
- Session length default = age band caps above; scale factors: `energy_high: -0` (keep short but more sessions), `fear/anxious: -30%`, `senior: -30%`, `adolescent: +0` but higher reinforcement rate. All low (convention).
- Frequency > duration: prefer multiple micro-sessions (convention; no trial evidence found).
- Reinforcement rate: puppies/adolescents high (continuous -> variable only after >=80% success; convention).
- Owner time budget: IF budget < 10 min/day THEN plan only the top-3 priorities of the band (socialization/handling/toileting for puppies; settle/recall/handling for adolescents). convention.
- Always attach the force-free requirement: no aversive tools (AVSAB #1; aversive methods associated with more problems #26, #27). high.

### 3.11 Breed/size modifiers (data only, low confidence)
- Large/giant: extend adolescence to ~24 mo; stricter joint protection (#36, #37); lower impact; slower neutering decisions (vet). Small/toy: earlier maturity; consider fear (Puurunen: small size associated with social fearfulness; #12); house training can be harder (convention - not verified).
- Breed group: use as prior for energy/arousal, not for age band.
- German Shepherd types: watch stranger aggression 6-12 mo (#22).

## 4. Safety / referral red flags (hard-coded blockers)
Route to veterinarian first: sudden behavior change at any age; house soiling in a previously reliable adult/senior; new aggression/fear/anxiety in seniors; night waking, disorientation, staring at walls; hearing/vision loss signs; limping, stiffness, reluctance to climb; vomiting/diarrhea/lethargy in puppies (parvo risk) - do not attend classes if unwell; incomplete vaccination + exposure to unknown dogs.
Route to veterinary behaviorist (DACVB), certified behavior consultant (CAAB/IAABC/CPDT-KA with force-free credentials): bites with skin puncture; growling/snapping with guarding or handling; aggression to children; freeze-then-bite; extreme fear/shutdown (not eating, hiding for days) past decompression; panic during alone time with self-injury; persistent problem >2 weeks despite plan; ex-breeding/hoarding dog with persistent fear; stranger-directed aggression rising in adolescence (#22, #28); suspected CCD with severe sleep-wake or aggression change.
Never output: dominance/alpha-roll, shock/prong/choke collars, flooding for fearful dogs, "let them work it out" with other dogs, forced socialization at parties/parks, punishment for accidents, physical corrections for mouthing (AVSAB #1).
Safety with children: supervision is mandatory; teach children not to hug/approach sleeping dogs (general guidance; no source retrieved).

## 5. Open questions / gaps
- Quantitative dose-response of socialization (how many exposures, how often) - unknown (#6, #11). The "20-30 items/week" target is invented as a UI default; label it so.
- Verified size-specific ages: skeletal maturity, adolescence end, and senior onset were not obtained from primary sources; need radiographic data (e.g., growth-plate closure studies) and breed-specific life-expectancy (e.g., Kennel Club/VetCompass).
- Session length/frequency by age: no trial evidence found; need a decision on whether engine exposes these as editable defaults. Study of "short frequent vs long" in dogs may exist (learning/spacing studies) - not searched.
- House-training timeline: need a primary study (VetCompass/Generation Pup supplementary) of age-at-reliable.
- Bite-inhibition: no primary developmental research; consider contacting IAABC/DACVB for consensus statement.
- Post-adoption: no RCT of decompression periods; whether 3-14 day "no-demands" improves outcomes is untested. Bohland sample is Ohio, COVID period, 99 dogs, attrition.
- Fear periods: only 1 small controlled dog-vs-wolf study (#21); breed-dependent onset claims were only seen in secondary summaries. Need check of the "breed-dependent onset of fear-related avoidance in puppies" paper (2015, Journal of Veterinary Behavior?) before using.
- Senior: no verified evidence for training protocols in CCD; CCDR/CADES cutoffs (CCDR >=? CADES >=?) not extracted (paywalled) - pull them from the primary articles before encoding thresholds.
- Items verified only through search summaries and not in the local text: AAHA 2019/2023 details, Yarborough effect sizes, Puurunen ORs, Howell, Bray 2022, Hargrave, Brühl, Wallis. PDFs could not be text-extracted (no pdftotext). Re-check before quoting numbers.
- Citation caveats: several items surfaced via search summaries have inconsistent details (e.g., Duxbury volume number; Stepita attender counts). Prefer DOI checks.
