### #1 AVSAB Position Statement on Puppy Socialization (c.2008, reprint 2014) - FULL
- Position statement (2 pages), no data. Key text: "first three months of life" is the primary window; expose puppies to "as many new people, animals, stimuli and environments as can be achieved safely and without causing over-stimulation manifested as excessive fear, withdrawal or avoidance"; socialization before full vaccination "should be the standard of care". Classes "as early as 7-8 weeks"; "minimum of one set of vaccines at least 7 days prior to the first class and a first deworming", up to date throughout. Avoid dog parks and non-sanitized high-traffic areas. Classes on cleanable (indoor) surfaces. Positive reinforcement only. Schedule solo play/naps in crate or pen ("may help prevent over attachment"). Continue variety through year 1 (cites Bradshaw 2002 for SRB). Puppies past 3 months: continue socializing; "owners of puppies displaying fear should seek veterinary guidance".
- Limits: unreferenced assertion that "behavioral issues, not infectious diseases, are the number one cause of death for dogs under three years"; no quantitative dose.
- Supports: vaccine gate (rule 3.2-3), abort rule (3.2-2), alone-time/crate priority (band pup_8_12w), force-free constant. Does not support "alpha roll not supported" wording specifically (that is a separate AVSAB 2014 dominance statement); the 2008 text only says positive, consistent training beats methods that "involve punishment and/or encourage human dominance".

### #2 Stepita, Bain, Kass 2013 JAAHA 49(2):95-100 - ABSTRACT (web)
- 21 clinics / 24 trainers, four US cities. **279 puppies attended classes; none suspected or diagnosed with CPV** (web summary of the PubMed/Europe PMC record). Conclusion: vaccinated attenders at no greater risk. LINKS says "only ~48 attenders" - contradicted (see section 2).

### #3 AAHA 2019 Canine Life Stage Guidelines (Creevy et al., JAAHA 55:267-290) - TARGETED (definitions, behavior, nutrition, reproduction, tables 1/2/4; parasite/dental/vaccine detail skimmed)
- Table 1 (verbatim): Puppy = "birth to cessation of rapid growth (~6-9 mo, varying with breed and size)"; Young adult = "cessation of rapid growth to completion of physical and social maturation, which occurs in most dogs by 3 to 4 yr"; Mature adult = "completion ... until the last 25% of estimated lifespan (breed and size dependent)"; Senior = "last 25% of estimated lifespan through end of life". Text: "estimates of inclusive ages are not uniform and therefore are not proposed for the life stages of mature adults and seniors"; life stages are "arbitrary"; periods "phase in and out gradually"; lifespan is "generally predicted by breed lifespan" (Table 3 points to lifespan databases).
- Behavior section: "The time period between ~6 mo and 3 yr of age can be the most challenging for owners as the dog matures socially and behaviorally." Puppies "generally do not 'outgrow' their problems"; exposure without evaluating response "may result in sensitization instead of positive socialization". "No medical reason to delay puppy classes or social exposure until the vaccination series is completed as long as exposure to sick animals is prohibited, basic hygiene is practiced" (quoting AAHA Behavior Guidelines). Core vaccines "finishing at 16-20 wk". Only one-third of puppies get exposure outside the home during critical periods (cites a survey). Referral to veterinary behaviorist for aggression, self-injury, profound phobias, multiple diagnoses, non-responders. Mentions "fear periods" as a topic to discuss with owners of young/mature adults.
- Sterilization Textbox 1: castration small (<45 lb projected) ~6 mo; large (>=45 lb) "wait until growth stops (~9-15 months)"; spay small before first heat (5-6 mo), large 5-15 mo. First estrus "varies among breeds from 4 months in some small breeds to 24 mo in some large breeds."
- **Answers to open items:** AAHA gives NO size-specific ages for adolescence end, skeletal maturity or senior onset. Only: rapid growth ends ~6-9 mo; growth complete ~9-15 mo (large males, in a sterilization context, not radiographic); social+physical maturity by 3-4 yr; senior = last 25% of expected lifespan.
- Table 2 extras: puppy exams every 3-4 wk, then 6-12 mo; behavior items per stage include "begin socializing and handling from neonate", "educate on sensitive periods", "discuss bite inhibition", "crate training relative to housetraining", "routine evaluation for cognitive changes" in seniors. Vaccination/dental/parasite sections contain nothing engine-relevant beyond core vaccines finishing 16-20 wk (rest read, no change).
- Supports: life-stage framework, 25% senior rule, vaccine/class rule, hygiene gating. Contradicts: band `adult_1_7y` (AAHA young adult runs to 3-4 yr, then mature adult); NOTES `mature_7_9y` and the size table of senior ages (no source).

### #4 AAHA 2023 Senior Care Guidelines (Dhaliwal et al., JAAHA 59:1-21) - TARGETED (definition, CCD, pain, nutrition; diagnostics tables skimmed)
- "Because of the variability in aging ... **there is no specific age for senior status**"; defines senior by the 2019 rule (last 25% of estimated lifespan). "Approximately 14-22.5% of dogs older than 8 yr suffer from age-related cognitive impairment" (cites ref 39). CCD "may be a diagnosis of exclusion"; DISHAA acronym = Disorientation, social Interactions, Sleep-wake, House-training loss and learned behaviors, Activity, Anxiety. CADES "is predictive of the progression of cognitive impairment"; serial owner-completed scores recommended. Selegiline is "the only drug labeled"; "effective in up to 70% of dogs" (cites ref 48); diets high in antioxidants/MCT "showed the most positive effect when combined with environmental enrichment (exercise, new toys, cognitive games, tasks, agility)"; other supplements/acupuncture "limited" rigor. Anxiety/pain are CCD components; clients mistake chronic pain for "normal aging"; senior hazards: stairs, pools, limited vision/hearing; medical work-up 1-2x/yr incl. thyroid.
- No CCDR/CADES numeric cutoffs. No statement about olfaction/scent-work.
- Supports: medical-first rules for seniors (3.6), 4-domain screen framing, supportive mode. Does not support: "AAHA sensory enrichment, olfaction retained" (NOTES 3.6b - not in text).

### #5 Asher et al. 2020 Biol Lett 16:20200097 - FULL
- Prospective cohorts of Guide Dogs UK candidates (Labrador, golden, GSD and crosses). (a) Puberty: 70 bitches; attachment/attention-seeking at 5 mo vs first proestrus relative to breed mean: R = -0.423 (n=64, p=0.0004, controlling diet and shared parentage); uncontrolled R = -0.315 (n=70, p=0.007). SRB at 5 mo: R = -0.295 controlled, -0.115 uncontrolled (ns). General anxiety not associated. (b) Obedience: 93 dogs, 'sit' by carer vs consistent stranger; n=82 at 5 mo, 80 at 8 mo, 69 both. Odds of repeatedly not responding at 8 vs 5 mo for carer: **OR 2.14, 95% CI 1.46-3.11, p = 0.044** (CI and p are internally awkward); no change for stranger. (c) Questionnaire trainability (n=285): carers scored lower at 8 mo than 5 mo (Z=-4.46) and higher at 12 than 5 (Z=13.76); **trainers (less familiar) scored trainability higher at 8 mo than 5 mo** (Z=5.42). C-BARQ separation-related behavior (SRB) **36% higher at 8 mo** than at 5 and 12 mo; SRB at 8 mo correlated with lower carer trainability (R=-0.516); attachment score unchanged by age.
- Limits: puberty assumed (not measured) in most dogs; first proestrus is a crude marker; bitches only; correlational; narrow breeds; guide-dog standardized rearing; authors flag age groupings "would need to be reconsidered for different breeds".
- Supports: adolescent `regression_tolerance` rule (3.4). Corrects: carer-specific, trainer-rated opposite direction. Not in paper: any specific "peak relinquishment ~8 mo" (it says adolescence "corresponds with the peak age at which dogs are relinquished", citing Weiss 2014/New 2000).

### #6 Howell, King, Bennett 2015 Vet Med Res Rep 6:143-153 - TARGETED
- Narrative review. Socialization practices "should begin within a few days of birth, and should extend well into adulthood"; breeder responsible until 8-12 wk; "evidence in support of puppy socialization classes is less clear ... some evidence ... positive ... other studies show no clear benefit"; "the minimum amounts ... are not known ... whether there is a maximum". By the juvenile period puppies in developed countries "will usually have received sufficient vaccinations to be able to go virtually anywhere". Puppy temperament tests give "mixed results".
- Also (middle sections): defines three periods (primary 0-3 wk, socialization ~3-12 wk and "certainly no later than 14", enrichment/juvenile); relays Scott & Fuller that **at 5 weeks puppies show a strong fear response to loud noises and unfamiliar environments** and fear of humans early in the period that fades with regular positive contact; breed differences in startle onset (3 wk Fox terriers/Beagles vs later Cocker/Shetland; all breeds by 4 wk); daily gentle handling in the first weeks -> calmer at 8 wk (esp. kennel-raised); puppies without litter-mates need particular care.
- Supports: lifelong socialization, dose unknown, class benefit unclear; does not give numbers.

### #7 Dietz, Arnold, Goerlich-Jansson, Vinke 2018 Behaviour 155:83-114 - ABSTRACT/portal summary only (paywalled)
- Review of maternal care, attachment formation and the sensitive socialisation period vs adult behavioural disorders. Conclusions (Utrecht portal): proper social stimulation in the sensitive period needed for normal development; negative experiences, insufficient stimulation and minimal maternal care/attachment raise disorder risk; evidence "still limited"; knowledge gaps listed. - Only a scanned-image PDF copy exists on the open web (no text layer; WebFetch and pypdf cannot read it); the University of Chicago Press page is a catalogue entry. Secondary descriptions used instead: Howell (#6) says Scott & Fuller reported puppies at **5 weeks "show a strong fear response toward all loud noises and unfamiliar environments"**, fear of humans early in the socialization period that disappears with regular positive contact, startle response developing at 3 wk in Fox terriers/Beagles but later in Cocker spaniels/Shetland Sheepdogs and all breeds by 4 wk; #11 gives the period boundaries and Fuller 1967's 2 x 20 min/wk. ### #9 Freedman, King, Elliot 1961 Science 133:1016-1017 - ABSTRACT/secondary only
- Record confirmed (PubMed 13701603). Puppies were kept with the mother in fenced fields from ~2-14 wk without routine human contact; per secondary descriptions the 7th week was the most receptive period, ~2.5/5-9/13 wk spans the human-socialization window, unsocialized puppies showed withdrawal that blocked normal bonds after 14 wk. Group sizes as reported in #11 (n=3-7 per age, 5 controls); a critique cited in search results calls the 9-wk group (n=3) "very small". No new numbers beyond #11.

### #10 Pfaffenberger & Scott 1959 J Genet Psychol 95(1):145-155 - NOT RETRIEVED (record only)
- DOI is 10.1080/00221325.1959.10534251 (LINKS gave ...10534294; unverified which is right). No abstract available; numbers used are #11's (12 wk ~90% success n=40; 14 wk slightly poorer n=22; >14 wk worse n=18). Magazine account says Pfaffenberger predicted guide-dog potential at 12 wk.

### #11 Canine socialisation narrative systematic review, Animals 2022;12(21):2895 - TARGETED (intro, methods, 4.x discussion, 5-6)
- Web of Science search (17 Aug 2022) -> 1,036 records -> 29 studies (14 questionnaire, 15 with experimental manipulation, 1 observational). Lists six periods: prenatal (9 wk), neonatal (0-2 wk), transition (2-3 wk), socialisation (3-12 wk), juvenile (12 wk-6 mo), pubertal (7-24 mo).
- Classic work as described: Pfaffenberger & Scott: homed at 12 wk (n=40) ~90% pass; at 14 wk (n=22) "slightly poorer ... not significantly"; kennelled >14 wk (n=18) "highly significant reduction". Freedman: weeks of human contact at 2 (n=6), 3 (6), 5 (7), 7 (7), 9 (3) wk + 5 controls; controls "tended to withdraw from human beings after 5 weeks"; the 9-wk group showed most attraction at 14-16 wk. Fuller 1967: lab beagles socialized with "as little as two 20-minute sessions of exposure per week"; Wolfe 1990 "<5 min human contact per week" gave "adequate" lab socialization - human-only, lab outcomes, "minimum levels ... not robustly investigated". Morrow 2015: Cavalier KCS had delayed onset vs Yorkshire terrier and GSD (breed-specific timing). Classic rules: ideal homing 6-8 wk; introduce to adult environment before 3-4 mo; Serpell et al. call "8 weeks" a possible misinterpretation. Puppy classes: Cutler 2017 benefits after 20 wk; Casey 2014 less aggression to strangers; Gonzalez-Martinez 2019 better family-dog aggression/trainability/non-social fear/touch; Kutsumi n=44/39/27/32; Seksel 1999 "very few benefits" (n=10-13/group, ages 6-17 wk); "every study investigating the effects of puppy classes on adult behaviour used an owner-filled questionnaire". Pierantoni: separation at 30-40 d -> more problems. Bio Sensor/ENS: "recent studies have not been able to replicate the success of the original Bar Harbor study".
- Limits: classic studies are isolation experiments (cannot separate isolation trauma from lack of exposure), small n, few breeds; most modern work is retrospective owner questionnaires. Authors: minimum/maximum socialisation and breed timing unknown.

### #12 Puurunen et al. 2020 Sci Rep 10:3527 - TARGETED
- Owner-filled survey, ~6,000 pet dogs (Finland), logistic regression of fear of dogs / strangers. Associated: urban environment, poor puppy socialisation, infrequent training/activities, **small size (small vs large OR 3.29; medium vs large 2.04)**, female sex, **neutering** (intact vs neutered male OR 0.45; intact vs neutered female OR 0.76), breed differences (Chihuahua vs Pembroke Welsh Corgi OR 8.64). Less than 1-2 h/day exercise associated with fear of dogs (OR ~1.5-1.6).
- Limits: cross-sectional; reverse causation (fearful dogs get less activity); no age-specific effects. Supports: socialization/activity priorities (low-med), size modifier "small = more social fear" (breed/size data only).

### #13 Tiira & Lohi 2015 PLoS ONE 10:e0141907 - TARGETED
- Finnish owner survey, n=3,264 (3,284 answers, 192 breeds; recruitment targeted at six breeds via breed clubs/Facebook). Fearful dogs: less socialization (p=0.002) and poorer owner-reported maternal care (p<0.0001) in the main (A) analysis; **in the comorbidity-controlled (B) analysis solely-fearful dogs did NOT differ in maternal care**, but did in socialization and (non-significant tendency) exercise. Noise sensitivity (p<0.0001) and separation anxiety (p=0.007; a single owner question) were most associated with **less daily exercise**; exercise was "not statistically significant" for fearfulness. Authors discuss reverse causation (anxious dogs walked less) and that exercise tracks overall management quality.
- Supports: activity floor rule (3.7) as association only; socialization checklist (low dose evidence).

### #14 Vaterlaws-Whiteside & Hartmann 2017 Appl Anim Behav Sci 197:55-61 - ABSTRACT (Purdue abstract page + library record)
- Standardized "nest" socialization program birth-6 wk, matched to developmental stage; **six litters** (puppy n not stated in abstract), comparison litters; outcomes: practical puppy assessment at 6 wk and handler questionnaire at 8 mo. More favorable: 6-wk assessment (P<<0.01); 8-mo separation-related behavior (P<<0.01), distraction (P<<0.01), general anxiety (P=0.02), body sensitivity (P=0.03); authors say benefits persisted through the first year. Secondary sources: ~5 min/pup/day rising to ~15 min by wk 5-6, five days/week (not confirmed in abstract). Few litters = low independence; handler-reported outcomes. Supports breeder-side advice only (R-HOMING / breeder content), low-med.

### #15 Bray et al. 2017 PNAS 114(34):9128-9133 - ABSTRACT (web search; PNAS page returned 403)
- **98 puppies** followed from birth (guide-dog candidates), ~70% program success; higher overall maternal behavior linked to greater chance of program failure, nursing styles requiring more effort from puppies linked to success; failure also linked to slow multistep-task solving with perseveration and more anxiety on a novel-object task. LINKS' "maternal investment in first 5 wk predicted success" is a looser paraphrase; direction is "more intense/effortful maternal style predicted success, over-solicitous care predicted failure". Small n, one organization.

### #16 Pierantoni et al. 2011 Vet Rec 169:468 - ABSTRACT (web): 140 adults, 70 adopted at 30-40 d vs 70 at ~2 mo; early group higher odds of destructiveness, excessive barking, fearfulness on walks, noise reactivity, possessiveness, attention-seeking; effect significant in pet-shop dogs, not other sources.

### #17 Puppies' age at adoption and adult behavior, Vet Sci 2025;12(2):176 - TARGETED
- Sardinian university-hospital screening clients, C-BARQ; **107 adult dogs (1-16 y)** grouped by age at adoption: <=1 mo n=39, <=2 mo n=33, <=3 mo n=23, >=4 mo n=12; origin (breeder/private/shelter) had no effect. Kruskal-Wallis: <=1 and <=2 mo groups scored higher on attachment/attention-seeking, and higher fear/anxiety than the >=4 mo group; nothing else significant.
- Limits: tiny >=4 mo group; recall of adoption age over years; "<=1 month" adoption (before ~5 wk) is a welfare-neglect case not a normal option. I found no explicit "homing at 2-3 mo" recommendation (LINKS claims one).

### #18 Santos, Beck, Fontbonne - maternal behaviour review, J Small Anim Pract 2019 online (61(2):85-92, 2020) - TARGETED

### #21 Lazzaroni et al. 2019 Front Psychol 10:2001 - TARGETED (intro, methods, results; discussion not read in full)
- Novel-object test at 6, 10, 14, 18, 22, 26 wk; **12 Alaskan husky puppies (2 litters) and 13 European gray wolves (3 litters), hand-raised from 10 days with 24 h human presence for 2 months, extensive socialization**; latency to approach (fear marker; correlated with fear behaviors rho=0.188). Dogs' latency to approach fell with age (slope 0.1065, CI 0.054-0.159) while wolves' did not (0.0192, CI -0.007-0.045); dogs approached faster than wolves at 18, 22, 26 wk. "No evidence in support of an increase in fearfulness in wolves with age or a delayed onset of fear response in dogs."
- Discussion (read): authors say fear was expressed as delayed approach, vanished after first contact with the object, no species difference at 6 or 10 wk, species difference only from 18 wk because dogs lose fear; they acknowledge small samples and "uncertainty in the magnitude of the effects" from individual variation and note differing sexual-maturity pace as a possible confound.
- Limits: 2 dog litters of one working type, intensively socialized, one novel object per age (different objects; added movement/sound at 22-26 wk), objects only (not people/noises), no pet-home puppies, ends at 26 wk. Cannot refute a fear period in ordinary pet puppies; shows it is not universal under heavy social exposure.

### #22 Foyer et al. 2016 Front Vet Sci 3:49 - TARGETED
- 978 prospective guide dogs (The Seeing Eye, US; GSD 276, golden 207, Lab 296, Lab x golden 219); C-BARQ at 6 and 12 mo (741 dogs for the change analysis); scores dichotomized (any >0). Change 6->12 mo: GSD stranger-directed aggression rise (breed x age p=0.018; but **GSD at 12 mo OR 1.68, 95% CI 0.95-2.97, p=0.073**); owner-directed aggression fell in all (OR 0.69, 0.58-0.84); separation-related behavior fell; non-social fear fell (females only). Environment (Bonferroni-significant): puppy frightened by a person -> stranger-directed fear **OR 4.60 (2.45-8.39)** and lower training success **OR 0.446 (0.245-0.812)**; threatened/attacked by unfamiliar dog -> dog-directed fear OR 1.59 (1.17-2.18) and stranger-directed aggression OR 1.61 (1.16-2.34); 5+ prior puppies raised by the raiser protective (stranger aggression OR 0.51); other dogs in the home -> lower owner-directed aggression OR 0.35 (0.23-0.54). Teenagers in home: higher dog-directed aggression/touch sensitivity (not significant after correction).
- Limits: retrospective raiser reports of events, correlational (fearful dogs may be more likely to be frightened), single organization. Supports 3.3 incident-recovery rule; GSD flag is weak (low).

### #23 Pre-adolescent training and canine behavior, Animals 2021;11(5):1298 - TARGETED
- Retrospective online survey (Center for Canine Behavior Studies members + social media): 641 owners, 1,023 dogs (median age 7 y, range 1-19); 48% attended puppy training <=6 mo; 99% of dogs had >=1 problem; house soiling 78%. Attending vs not: aggression OR 0.71 (0.53-0.97), compulsion 0.64 (0.45-0.92), destruction 0.60 (0.37-0.96), excessive barking 0.68 (0.47-0.99); **house soiling OR 1.56 (1.08-2.27) (worse)**. Start age (<=3 vs 4 vs 5-6 mo), number of classes and equipment: no effect. Reward-based training: aggression OR 0.52 (0.28-0.96). Acquired <=12 wk: fear/anxiety OR 0.65, destruction 0.50. Neutered: fear/anxiety OR 3.10, coprophagia 2.01, escaping 1.97. No multiple-comparison adjustment.
- Limits: convenience sample, recall of puppyhood up to 19 y earlier, binary outcomes, no severity; 28% of "reward-based" owners also used punitive devices.

### #24 Kinsman et al. 2025 Animals 15(8):1163 (Generation Pup) - TARGETED (results + discussion complete)
- Free-text "behaviours you find a problem" at 6, 9, 12, 15, 18 mo; UK/ROI puppies enrolled <16 wk; one puppy per household; surveys available: 1,718 / 1,451 / 1,219 / 823 / 684 (not the same dogs at each point; **n=1,219 is the 12-month survey, not the cohort**). >=1 undesirable behavior: **29.9% (27.7-32.1), 34.0, 42.1% (39.3-44.9), 35.1, 32.0%**; median 1 behavior/dog. Barking 7.9->13.4 (12 mo)->10.5%; jumping up 5.2->7.9->5.6; lead pulling 3.8->6.6->3.4; recall 3.3->6.6->2.8 (barking, pulling, recall differ significantly, jumping up does not); contact behaviours 5.1->3.5; mouthing people 1.6->0.3; toileting 2.3->0.7; destructive 3.1->1.3. **Rising with age:** displaying fear 1.8->3.9 (12)->3.2%; "aggressive" wording 0.9->3.4% (18 mo); reactive 0.5->1.9%; lunging 0.5->0.9-1.2; chasing 1.2->2.5. Authors: pubertal period 6-12 mo (breed-dependent), 1-2 y post-pubertal; unknown whether declines are developmental or owner training/habituation.
- Limits: owner-perceived free text, social-desirability under-reporting of aggression, loss to follow-up, UK (pre-Mar 2020) puppy-class-heavy cohort.
- Supports: adolescent peak ~12 mo, easing by 15-18 mo for training-type problems; NOT "everything fades" (fear/aggression/reactivity wording rises).

### #25 Generation Pup puppy class attendance, Animals 2025 - TARGETED
- n=2,187; **67.1% (1,468) attended >=1 class by the 16-wk survey** (table also shows 67.5%); odds up with income, prior intention, first-time ownership, puppy pack; down with later acquisition age. Last-class description (n=1,468): puppies "did not play together" **41.7%**, all played 33.5%, some played 24.8%; mixed sizes 63.5%; vet-run 19.5%; house-training advice not given in 57% (Figure 1); 6.5% had >=11 dogs. Non-attenders (n=719; 519 answered): want to train themselves 57.4%, "no suitable classes" 40.9%, no time 11.6%.
- The paper reports whether puppies played, not that play was "not allowed"; it does not judge no-play classes as poor quality.

### #26 Generation Pup separation-related behaviour at 6 mo, Anim Welf 2024 - TARGETED
- Exploratory risk-factor analysis, **n=145 of 1,987 (1,218 excluded for missing data, 462 non-fitting)**; 46.9% (68) met SRB case definition (alone-only signs). Multivariable ORs (very wide CIs): overnight enclosure <=16 wk OR 0.029 (0.00-0.24); 9 h sleep OR 0.142 (0.03-0.72), 10+ h OR 0.019 (0.00-0.16); **>=4 accidents/wk at <=16 wk OR 13.25 (2.49-70.37)**; >=2 aversive techniques vs <=1 (no attention scenario) OR 11.09 (1.72-71.54); "fussing" on return OR 5.76 (1.24-26.71); owner aged 16-34 OR 11.12; non-standard kibble as reward OR 13.85; time-out methods OR 97.96 (8.36-1148.5) in one scenario. Authors: correlational, confirmation needed.

### #27 Pandemic Puppies 21-month problem behaviours, Animals 2024 - TARGETED
- Of n=788 who answered all 24 problem-behavior items, **762 (96.7%) reported >=1** (median 5, IQR 3-7); one third SRB; **82.3% had used >=1 aversive method** (n=898: 17.7% none, 18.9% one, 22.0% two, 39.0% >2; commonest "physically moving the dog"); aversive use associated with more problem behaviors (direction unknowable). Only attendance at online puppy classes <16 wk predicted lower aversive use. About a third underestimated training difficulty (more in first-timers).
- Limits: 24 very inclusive items (barking, jumping, etc.), pandemic cohort, associational.

### #28 Bohland et al. 2023 PLoS ONE 18:e0289356 - FULL
- **What it measured:** prospective observational cohort; adopters of 99 dogs from 5 Ohio shelters (Oct 2020-Jun 2021, COVID period) answered the 42-item short C-BARQ (0-4 Likert; "not formally validated") at 7, 30, 90, 180 d plus satisfaction and household-change items; shelter-record predictors (age category <6 mo / 6 mo-1.9 y / 2-6.9 y / >=7 y, weight, days in shelter, intake type, psychotropic/GI/antibiotic meds, prior return, first-time owner). Mixed linear models with dog and shelter random effects. Respondents 99 -> 83 -> 75 -> 83 (62.6% completed all; a survey-coding error stopped later mailings for non-responders in the first half). Dog mean 3.0 y, 41.7 lb, 35% received behavior meds in shelter, 100% neutered, mostly mixed breed.
- **Findings (change vs day 7, coefficient on 0-4 scale):** stranger-directed aggression +0.25 (30 d), +0.44 (90 d), +0.44 (180 d), all p<=0.005; chasing +0.24/+0.56/+0.64; training difficulty +0.19/+0.23/+0.28; excitability +0.42 (90 d), +0.27 (180 d); touch sensitivity +0.41 (90 d), +0.25 (180 d); separation-related -0.23 (180 d only); attachment/attention-seeking -0.31 (180 d). No change: owner/dog/familiar-dog aggression, stranger-directed fear, nonsocial fear, dog-directed fear, energy. Shelter psychotropic use predicted stranger-directed aggression (+0.41) and touch sensitivity (+0.42). Dogs >7 y: lower training difficulty (-0.57); >56 lb higher. **Prevalence figures in the abstract (e.g., stranger-directed aggression 81.7%, nonsocial fear 95.8%, separation 92.6%) are the highest across timepoints of "any item score >0", not rates of problems.** At 180 d: 100% adjusted extremely/moderately well, 93.7% good/excellent, 75% improved, 3.8% worsened; 7 returns (7.1%) vs ~15% US average cited.
- Authors' caveats: increases may reflect owners observing more behavior (nail trims, freedom to chase), shelter "behavioral blunting", territorial items rising as dogs settle, COVID lockdown effects; sampling bias; no analysis of treatment. "Honeymoon period" is the paper's framing; **3-3-3 is never mentioned** (confirmed by search).
- Conflicts with Powell 2022 (#29), who found training difficulty and nonsocial fear decreased over 4 mo using C-BARQ.
- Supports: "expect change, not just improvement" (3.5, medium); check-ins at 7/30/90/180 d. Weakens: using the changes as clinical alarm thresholds.

### #31 Vitulova et al. 2018 Acta Vet Brno 87:155-163 - TARGETED

### #37/#38 Hart et al. 2020 (Front Vet Sci 7:388 and 7:472) - TARGETED (abstract/intro)

### #39 Salvin et al. 2011 Vet J 188:331 (CCDR) - ABSTRACT (web)

The abstract reports no score cutoffs; primary full text (ScienceDirect) returned 403.

### #41 Yarborough et al. 2022 Sci Rep 12:13316 (Dog Aging Project) - TARGETED
- n=15,019 US dogs (mean age 6.9, SD 4.2), owner-completed CCDR-derived "CSLB" scale; **CCD = score >=50: 1.4% of all dogs**. **Age OR 1.68/year unadjusted (1.60-1.77); 1.52/year adjusted (1.44-1.61), over the whole age range** (not "after age 10"); not active vs very active OR 6.47 (adjusted); neurological, eye or ear disorder history raises odds. - Limits: owner-report; sensory impairment can inflate scores (authors); no clinical diagnosis. Relevant to size-specific senior definition: they operationalized "last quartile" by weight, not by size bands.

## 3. Evidence-graded rules (recommended)

Confidence: H = multiple converging sources or authoritative statement; M = one decent source or consistent expert consensus; L = single weak study. `CONV` = convention, no study supports the number; keep in a tunable constants file.

### 3.1 Life-stage bands

| band | age rule | rationale / source | conf |
|---|---|---|---|
| puppy_early | 7-12 wk (7 only if class-eligible) | AVSAB classes from 7-8 wk after >=1 vaccine set >=7 d earlier + deworm; socialisation period 3-12 (14 per Purdue/Houpt) wk; AAHA puppy stage to rapid-growth end | H (structure), CONV (numbers) |
| puppy_late | 12-16 wk | vaccines "finishing at 16-20 wk" (AAHA); continue socialisation | M |
| juvenile | 16 wk - 6 mo | #11 juvenile period 12 wk-6 mo; AAHA puppy stage ends ~6-9 mo | M |
| adolescent | 6-18 mo default; large/giant extend to 24 mo (CONV) | Asher dip at 8 mo; Kinsman peak 12 mo, easing 15-18; #11 pubertal 7-24 mo; AAHA 6 mo-3 yr "most challenging"; first estrus up to 24 mo in large breeds; **no size-specific study** | M (existence), CONV (end ages) |
| young_adult | end of adolescence to 3-4 yr "social maturity" (soft) | AAHA Table 1 | M |
| mature_adult | until last 25% of expected lifespan | AAHA 2019 | H (rule), no ages |
| senior | last 25% of expected lifespan (needs expected_lifespan input) | AAHA 2019/2023; Yarborough weight-based quartile approach | H (rule) |
| senior_screening_trigger | any dog >=7 y OR >=75% of expected lifespan: ask sensory/mobility/cognition | Brühl geriatric >=7; Hopper/AAHA 8-y risk jump; Wallis owners old at ~10-11 | M |

Fallback expected lifespan if unknown (CONV, flagged): do NOT use fixed senior ages; if an estimate is required offer size defaults as editable: toy/small 13-14 y, medium 12, large 10-11, giant 8-9 -> senior start = 0.75 x lifespan (about 10, 9, 7.5-8, 6-7). These are inferred, not in any source; owners' perceived onset (10-11) and vets' (8, medium) bracket them.

## 4. Gaps & open questions

- Size-specific adolescence end, skeletal maturity and senior onset: **no primary source on file.** Needed: radiographic physeal-closure data by weight class; breed longevity tables (VetCompass/Kennel Club/Urfer et al. mean life expectancy by weight, sex, neuter - the table Yarborough used). Until then expose expected lifespan as an input and keep age defaults flagged CONV.
- CADES cutoffs now confirmed by two PMC papers (0-7 / 8-23 / 24-44 / 45-95) and the 42%/24% conversion figures by the Madari abstract; the primary Madari table is still unread (ScienceDirect 403) - confirm if licensing allows. CCDR "at risk" 40 is inferred; CCDR sensitivity/specificity not in the abstract (Yarborough: "difficult to obtain").
- Still unreadable/unverified after retrieval attempts: #8 Scott & Fuller (image-only scan), #10 Pfaffenberger (record only; DOI discrepancy), #20 Battaglia (secondary only), #9 Freedman and #7 Dietz (abstract/summary), Milgram 2002, and all paywalled abstracts (#2, #14, #15, #16, #34, #35, #47, #49, #51), several of which came from web summaries rather than the journal abstract text. Numbers from these are indicative only.
- No trial of decompression/no-demands period, of session length by age, of socialization dose, or of house-training age-to-reliability; Bohland and Powell disagree on whether training difficulty rises or falls after adoption.
- Is the owner-reported adolescent peak at 12 mo (Kinsman, free-text, mixed breeds) vs 8 mo (Asher, guide dogs, 'sit' test) the same phenomenon? Different outcomes; engine should treat 6-18 mo as one band with a bumpy dip, not two sharp dates.
- Puppy-class evidence is owner-questionnaire only (#11) and includes a harmful signal for house soiling in #23; effect of "no puppy play" classes unknown.
- Tiira, Puurunen, Yarborough, Bray, Foyer and Gen Pup results are all associational; use as priorities/flags, never as clinical claims.
- Fear-period timing: Lazzaroni covers 12 hand-raised huskies; Morrow 2015 (breed-specific onset, cited in #11) not read.
