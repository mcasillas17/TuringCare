## 1. Per-source digests

### 1.1 Breed, genetics, size

**#1 Morrill K et al. 2022, Science 376:eabk0639 (PMC9675396). Tier W.**
- Design: Darwin's Ark citizen-science cohort. Owner survey of **18,385 dogs (49% purebred)**; DNA sequenced for 2,155 dogs; 85% US participants. Breed effect measured as generalized eta-squared (ges) in ANOVA on confirmed purebreds, and marginal R-squared in linear mixed models on mixed breeds (restricted to dogs with <45% ancestry from any one breed).
- Verified quotes: "Breed explains just 9% of behavioral variation in individuals." Breed effect "about fivefold higher for the physical traits characteristic of breeds than for behavioral traits." "only 5.1% (30/583) of breed-phenotype pairs were significantly differentiated for behavioral questions ... compared with 41.5% (17/41 pairs) for physical traits". "67.2 +/- 7.5% within one SD and 95.4 +/- 3.0% within two SD for confirmed purebred dogs". Age ges 0.018, sex ges 0.009 (except "lifts leg to urinate", 0.48), size "virtually no effect" (6.6e-4 +/- 8.6e-4). Retrieving heritability 52.5 +/- 9.2%, human sociability factor 67.3 +/- 13.0%. Golden retriever example: "increases from 22% for a mutt to 40% if that dog is a golden retriever" (top quartile of human sociability). 11 significant GWAS regions (136 suggestive), not independently replicated.
- Important details NOTES omits: mean breed ges **0.089 +/- 0.039 across 78 breeds, range 0.034 to 0.253** (so the 9% is an average; some behaviors are breed-heavy). In mutts, breed ancestry marginal R2 = 9 +/- 3%.
- Limitations (authors): owner reports are "susceptible to rater bias, including the influence of breed stereotypes"; survey data cannot separate environment from genetics; reference panels are not representative of each breed; volunteers can be self-selected (cohort mostly US).
- Supports: R1 (breed = weak prior), R2, R3 (owners identified only ~21% of a mutt's breed ancestry, so owner "breed guesses" for mixes are poor), R6/R7 (size and sex add little once breed is in the model). Does NOT support any specific 0.25/0.75 weight (see section 3).

**#2 MacLean EL, Snyder-Mackler N, vonHoldt BM, Serpell JA 2019, Proc R Soc B 286:20190716 (PMC6790757). Tier W.**
- Design: 14 C-BARQ traits, "more than 14,000 dogs from 101 breeds" (analysed 12,806 dogs/86 breeds and 13,907/98 breeds depending on the genetic panel); genetic data from 5,697 dogs.
- Verified: mean among-breed heritability **0.51 (SD 0.12), range 0.27 to 0.77**; trainability 0.73, stranger-directed aggression 0.68, chasing 0.62, attachment/attention-seeking 0.56. Defined as "the proportion of trait variance attributable to genetic similarity among breeds". The authors say the breed-level approach "does not provide resolution at the level of the individual" and that among-breed estimates were about 5x within-breed estimates.
- Supports: breed differences are real at breed-mean level (R4 direction), and also the caution in R1/R2. Does not contradict Morrill (different estimand: variance among breed means vs variance among individuals).

**#3 Dutrow EV, Serpell JA, Ostrander EA. bioRxiv 2022.04.26.489536 (preprint; local PDF) / Cell 185:4737 (PMC10478034). Tier F + W.**
- Design: 4,261 genotyped canids (2,823 purebred, 226 FCI breeds; 687 mixed; 658 village dogs; 93 wild canids), 10 lineages by PHATE embedding + pseudotime; C-BARQ for **46,973 dogs across 237 breeds (mean 136/breed)**; correlation analyses on **132 breeds with >=30 dogs** (dingo and African/Middle Eastern lineages dropped). Two-sided **Spearman** rho between breed-average pseudotime and breed-average C-BARQ factor score, per lineage and factor. GWAS on 1,171 WGS samples.
- Key results (same in preprint and published text): herder lineage vs non-social fear rho=0.64 (p=1.86e-5); terrier vs predatory chasing rho=0.69 (p=1.96e-5); scent hound vs trainability rho=-0.68 (p=4.5e-4). Trainability higher in working/sporting breeds (ANOVA p adj 3.06e-4 and 8.27e-3). Terriers and scent hounds cluster on lower trainability, higher touch sensitivity, familiar-dog aggression, non-social fear. **Sighthound lineage "showed no robust behavioral correlations (p > 0.01)"**. Border collie sheepdog haplotype near EPHA5: 77.5% in border collies, 60% in show vs 83.3% in working lines.
- Limitations (authors): C-BARQ "designed to measure broad aspects of temperament ... rather than expressly targeting breed-typical behavioral functions"; breed averages remove individual variation by design; no structural variants. Preprint text internally inconsistent (says "significant correlations for all eight lineages" and later that sighthounds show none).
- Not in the files: Table S3 (all rho values). NOTES says "r"; these are Spearman rho on breed means (n roughly 20 to 40 breeds per lineage).
- Supports: R4 herder non-social fear, terrier chasing, scent-hound lower trainability (breed-mean level only), R5 (working vs show line shifts haplotype frequency). Contradicts NOTES R4 "terrier trainability -0" (the paper says terriers cluster on lower trainability) and "sighthound chasing +1" (no support; lineage showed nothing behavioral). Herder "energy +1" and "chasing/motion-reactivity +1": **not in the text** (energy/excitability appear only as an axis of the UMAP).
- Licensing: preprint is CC-BY-NC-ND; irrelevant to the engine except for quoting.

**#4 McGreevy PD et al. 2013, PLoS One 8:e80529. Tier F.**
- Design: breed-level. C-BARQ (n=8,301 dogs, **49 breeds, >=30 dogs each**) linked to breed height (ANKC standards; six breeds from a web portal), breed mean bodyweight (owner-reported in C-BARQ) and cephalic index from photos of 6M+6F show-quality adults per breed. Weighted stepwise backward regression per trait; adjusted R2 reported.
- Results: 33 of 36 traits had at least one significant predictor (height alone 14, bodyweight alone 5, CI alone 3, weight+CI 2, height+CI 3, height+weight 6). All height coefficients negative except trainability (positive). Strong (p<0.001) inverse height relationships: mounting, touch sensitivity, urination/defecation when left alone, dog-directed fear, separation problems, non-social fear, owner-directed aggression, begging, urine marking, attachment/attention-seeking. Bodyweight inverse with excitability and hyperactivity. CI positive with self- and allo-grooming, negative with chasing (p=0.020). Short-skulled: more compulsive staring, allo-grooming. Height-weight correlation 0.928.
- Limitations (authors): owner reports and attention bias; "some of the trends ... may be the result of nurture rather than nature"; owners may tolerate/indulge small dogs; breed history (terriers, bull types, miniatures) may confound; terriers under-represented; CI from show dogs in Australia while behavior from an international pet sample.
- C-BARQ "trainability" description includes "responds positively to correction" (an aversive-method item).
- Supports R6 direction (small = more attachment/touch/separation; large = more trainable) at breed level; R14 (skull shape). Weak for individual prediction; Morrill finds size has "virtually no effect" within breed.

**#13 Ilska J et al. 2017, Genetics 206:675. Tier F-skim (XML).**
- Design: **1,975 UK Labrador Retrievers** with C-BARQ data; pedigree of 28,943 dogs; 885 genotyped. REML animal models (heritability), genomic h2, GWAS.
- Results: pedigree h2 from 0.03 (SE 0.04) owner-directed aggression (not significant) to 0.38 (SE 0.08) fetching; noise fear 0.30 (0.08), stranger/non-owner aggression 0.26 to 0.29, non-social fear 0.25, trainability 0.28 when fetching is separate, excitability 0.10 to 0.11, attention 0.14, separation anxiety not significant. Genomic h2 mostly lower (0.00 for attention/excitability, 0.31 for noise fear). "mainly polygenic".
- Limitations: one breed, owner-reported, small SEs wide for genetic correlations, effects of 0.1-0.4.
- Supports NOTES statement (#13). Note NOTES wording "negligible for others" is right (owner aggression 0.03). Adds that within-breed h2 is moderate (0.25-0.38) for fear/aggression/fetching, which is not trivial.

**#14 Fadel FR et al. 2016, Sci Rep 6:22162. Tier F.**
- Design: DIAS owner survey; 1,734 responses, 1,495 usable, **1,161 purebred (716 Border Collies, 445 Labradors)**; MANOVA + Mann-Whitney.
- Results: DIAS structure replicated (3 factors); Cronbach alpha overall 0.73, behavioural regulation 0.82, aggression threshold/response to novelty 0.71, responsiveness 0.45; "easy to train" did not load on factor 1 and "appears to be sorry" loaded nowhere (dropped). Line (work vs show) significant for overall impulsivity, regulation, responsiveness; breed significant for aggression threshold/novelty. "The effect size of all these tests was small ... Partial Eta Squared < 0.06". Neutered males higher impulsivity/regulation/novelty score and lower responsiveness than entire males; no difference in females. "more differences WITHIN breeds than BETWEEN breeds" (line comparisons).
- Limitations: only two breeds; self-selected owners; line classified by owner; neutering effect confounded.
- Supports R5 only for these two breeds (extrapolation to "sporting/herding breeds" is not shown). R20 (DIAS) with caveats on responsiveness subscale reliability (0.45).

### 1.2 Population surveys (Finnish)

**#16 Salonen M et al. 2020, Sci Rep 10:2962. Tier F.** 13,715 owner responses, 264 breeds, mean age 4.7 y. 72.5% had some highly problematic behaviour; noise sensitivity 32%, fear 29% (dogs 17%, strangers 15%, novel situations 11%), surfaces/heights 23.5%, inattention ~20%, hyperactivity/impulsivity 15%, compulsion 16%, aggression 14% (family 6%, strangers 6%), **separation-related 5%** (authors: prevalence "2-3 times higher" in other studies because only high-frequency dogs were counted). Comorbidity risk ratios: separation-related with hyperactivity RR 4.1 and inattention 3.4; aggression with fear 3.2; separation with fear 2.8. Males more aggressive/hyperactive, females more fearful; noise sensitivity (esp. thunder) rises with age; hyperactivity falls with age. Breed examples: 10.6% of Miniature Schnauzers aggressive to strangers vs 0.4% of Labradors. Convenience sample; thresholds ("high") are authors' own. NOTES says "separation ~5-6%": text gives 5% in results and 6% in discussion; fine.

**#17 Sulkama S et al. 2021, Transl Psychiatry 11:501. Tier F.** n=11,539 (hyperactivity/impulsivity) and 11,164 (inattention). Associated with younger age (F=295.9), male (z=8.27), medium size > small (z=4.74) and > large (z=2.78), <1 h exercise/day vs >3 h (z=4.75), alone >8 h vs <3 h (z=3.99), **not the owner's first dog** (z=4.65), plus compulsion, aggression, fearfulness (all p<0.0001). Highest H/I breeds Cairn Terrier, Jack Russell, German Shepherd, Staffordshire Bull Terrier; lowest Chinese Crested, Rough Collie, Chihuahua. Self-selected convenience sample; authors say causality can only be speculated (owners may not train inattentive dogs). No effect sizes beyond test statistics. Note: small dogs had the LOWEST H/I here, which conflicts with R6 "<10 kg excitability +0.5" (McGreevy 2013 says lighter dogs more excitable; breed-level).

**#18 Mikkola S et al. 2021, Sci Rep 11:9433. Tier F.** 9,270 purebred dogs (1,791 "high aggression toward people", 7,479 "low"); logistic regression. Higher odds with older age, male, **high fearfulness (>5x odds vs not fearful)**, small size, being the only dog in the household, first-time owner; breeds Rough Collie, Miniature Poodle, Miniature Schnauzer highest vs Labrador (pairwise ORs 5.1-5.4). Cannot separate fear-based from other aggression. Supports "fear is the strongest correlate of aggression" and R9/R24; the odds-ratio table is garbled in text extraction (only text-stated values used).

**#19 Hakanen E et al. 2020, Sci Rep 10:13674. Tier F.** n=9,613 (fireworks), 9,513 (thunder), 6,945 (novel situations), 2,932 (surfaces). Less socialisation, neutering, inexperienced owner, no dog companions, less training/activities, urban living, small size associated with more fear (e.g., fireworks: seldom-trained vs weekly OR 1.41; only dog OR 1.44; first-dog OR 1.53; neutered vs intact OR 0.75 for intact). Fear of fireworks/thunder **rises to ~10 y then falls**. Cross-sectional; direction of causality unknown. Supports R19, R24, and suggests R8 (senior noise sensitivity) is not monotonic.

**#20 Puurunen J et al. 2020, Sci Rep 10:3527. Tier F.** n=5,973 (fear of dogs) and 5,932 (fear of strangers). Poor puppy socialisation (7-16 weeks; chi2=82.9 and 129.7), breed, small size (small vs large OR 3.29; small vs medium 2.04 for fear of dogs), urban environment, less training/exercise (<1 h vs >3 h OR 1.58), neutered vs intact (males OR 0.45 for intact vs neutered; females 0.76), intact females more fearful than intact males. A priori breed group (Chihuahua, JRT, Lagotto, Shetland) vs (GSD, Golden, Labrador, Staff) OR 1.57/1.65. Socialisation recalled retrospectively by owners (recall bias).

**#21 Tiira K, Lohi H 2015, PLoS One 10:e0141907. Tier F.** n=3,262 dogs >6 months (3,284 responses, 192 breeds). Fearful dogs: poorer maternal care (p<0.0001) and less socialisation (p=0.0002); noise sensitivity: daily exercise was the largest factor (F=22.07, p<0.0001) plus sterilised, owner's first dog, older, less time alone; separation anxiety also associated with less exercise (p=0.007). Authors note owners who exercise more also engage more with dogs; exercise variable is minutes x leash/free multiplier. Cross-sectional.

### 1.3 Neutering and sex

**#22 McGreevy PD et al. 2018, PLoS One 13:e0196284. Tier F-skim.** 6,235 male dogs castrated for reasons other than behavior (2,712 excluded). Percentage lifetime exposure to gonadal hormones (PLGH). Of 100 C-BARQ behaviours, 40 differed >5% in proportion of "high tendency" dogs between entire and castrated; 25 related to PLGH and 14 to age at castration. Longer PLGH associated with **fewer** reports of 26 behaviours (8 fear, 7 aggression), but **more** indoor marking (OR 1.011) and howling alone (OR 1.004), and **better** recall/fetching *when castrated earlier* (recall OR 0.997/1% PLGH). Fear of noise: OR 0.996 per 1% (0.963 per 10%, 0.911 per 25%). The "5.04% to 12.31%" range is the **difference in the proportion of dogs** reported high on a behaviour, not an effect size per unit. Authors: cannot infer causation; C-BARQ completers may be more engaged; breeds mature at different ages.

**#23 Starling M et al. 2019, PLoS One 14:e0223709. Tier F-skim.** 8,981 females spayed for non-behavioral reasons (1,552 excluded). 23 of 100 behaviours differed >5%; 10 behaviours related to PLGH or age at spay; differences 5.33%-7.22%. Fear of aggressive unfamiliar dogs OR 0.997/1% PLGH (0.963 per 10%). Higher PLGH: more excitability at doorbell/car trips (ORs 0.989/0.997 for age at spay), more howling (1.004) and chewing (1.004). Authors: "we cannot determine a cause-effect relationship".

**#24 Farhoody P et al. 2018, Front Vet Sci 5:18. Tier F.** 15,370 surveys; 13,795 (familiar people), 13,498 (strangers), 13,237 (dogs). No association with aggression to familiar people/dogs; for strangers "dogs gonadectomized at 7-12 months of age ... were 26% more likely"; authors flag "a type I error or false-positive finding related to the use of two different models". Advocacy note: the discussion argues for vasectomy/hysterectomy because of health risks; author affiliation Zink Integrative Sports Medicine.

**#25 Torres de la Riva G et al. 2013, PLoS One 8:e55937. Tier F.** 759 Golden Retrievers 1-8 y at UC Davis hospital (145 intact males, 178 early-neutered males, 72 late males; 122/172/70 females). Early (<12 mo) neutering: hip dysplasia 10.3% vs 5.1% intact (males, p<0.01); CCL tear 5.1% (males) and 7.7% (females) vs 0 in intact; lymphosarcoma ~3x in early-neutered males; late-neutered females had higher HSA/MCT. Single-referral-hospital records; breed-specific.

### 1.4 Health, pain, medical

**#26 Mills DS et al. 2020, Animals 10:318 (PMC7071134). Tier W.** Narrative review. Table 1 (the authors' own ~100 referral dog cases across clinics): suspected-pain prevalence Bristol 68%, Lincoln 82%, Pennsylvania A 23%, Pennsylvania B 29%, Missouri/Illinois 79%, Barcelona 28%. Summary says "28-82%", abstract says "around a third", table minimum is 23%. The **16%-79% is the share of suspected-pain cases with some diagnostic confirmation** (Barcelona 16%, others 56-79%), not prevalence. Earlier cited estimates include Voith 5%, Mills 12% (dogs), unpublished 23%. Authors: "some of these associations are simply observed correlations"; "Trial analgesia may not be sufficient to definitively confirm a pain focus"; recommendations partly rest on the precautionary principle. Behaviours listed: defensive aggression, poor learning/performance, house-soiling, clinginess, star-gazing, fly-snapping, excessive licking, pica, repetitive limb licking, destructiveness when alone, unexplained fear/anxiety, resource guarding, refusing/freezing on walks; adjunct signs: abnormal gait, weight shifting, freezing, unusual posture, scratching/nibbling an area, restlessness, yawning, shaking. Supports R10/R11/R13 as a precautionary gate; evidence quality low (clinic series).

**#27 Camps T, Amat M, Manteca X 2019, Animals 9:1133. Tier F-skim.** Narrative review "based on our experience". Pain strongest; thyroid: "this relationship remains controversial"; but "all cases of aggressive-related problems should be checked for hypothyroidism ... the only way to rule out hypothyroidism ... is blood testing"; overt hypothyroidism supplementation "recommended and often solves or improves" behaviour; euthyroid dogs with low T4 should not be treated. Neurological: silent-zone tumours, TIA, mild TBI, idiopathic epilepsy (partial seizures can present as behaviour; 69% of 48 dogs had first seizure by 3 y, overlapping the usual age of onset 12-24 months); liver (portosystemic shunt) "can lead to behavioral problems that are very difficult to recognize"; "age of onset of the problem could be used ... as an indicator that more invasive ... work-up is justified" (the study they cite: dogs aged 5-15 y). Also covers vomeronasal and cardiac topics. No numeric age threshold. Supports R10/R12; the "~5-6 y" cutoff is not from this paper.

**#28 Lopes Fagundes AL et al. 2018, Front Vet Sci 5:17. Tier F.** Perspective, qualitative content analysis of **20 case records** (10 "clinical" with musculoskeletal pain focus, 10 matched controls) from one UK referral clinic. "age of onset ... was on average nearly 4 years later" (mean age at presentation 5y7m vs 4y7m); loud noises the primary complaint in 10/10 vs 6/10; generalisation to the wider environment in 8/10 vs 2/10 (5 to the location, 3 to avoiding the car). 9/10 neutered in both groups. Authors: exploratory; "clinical cases responded well to treatment once pain was identified". The specific "6.5 vs 2.8 y" in NOTES is **not in the text** (could be a figure/table not extractable).

**#37 Packer RMA et al. 2015, PLoS One 10:e0137496. Tier F.**
- Study 1: 700 dogs (97 breeds, mean age 5.2 y) at a referral hospital; **70 (10%) had BOAS across ALL breeds** (35 formally diagnosed by anaesthetised exam; 35 by owner-reported-breathing score >8/40 plus nares ratio <0.30). Study 2: 154 brachycephalic dogs from breeders (79%), first-opinion (14%), rescue (7%); **59 (38.3%) affected** (clinical exam before/after a 5-minute walk; no anaesthetised exam).
- "BOAS only occurred in dogs whose muzzles comprised less than half their cranial lengths" (craniofacial ratio CFR <0.5; the longest-muzzled case was a Staffordshire Bull Terrier at 0.49). Over 80% of dogs with CFR <0.1 affected; **no dog with CFR >=0.5**. Thicker neck girth and (Study 2) higher body condition score and neutering also associated. Pugs with CFR 0.03 predicted risk 0.95 vs 0.48 at CFR 0.21; +1 BCS in an average Pug raised predicted risk 0.93 to 0.98; neck girth -5 cm lowered 0.93 to 0.89 (effects "relatively subtle compared with CFR"). Highest-risk breeds Pug, French Bulldog, Bulldog; affected breeds also include Boston, Japanese Chin, Pekingese, Dogue de Bordeaux, Griffon Bruxellois, Boxer, Shih Tzu, Chihuahua, CKCS, Affenpinscher, Staffordshire Bull Terrier.
- Limitations (authors): no internal airway exam for most dogs; owners may be "dishonest" about prior surgery; neutering finding may be circular (show/breeding dogs removed from breeding when affected); obesity could be a consequence of BOAS. 18 dogs (Study 1) and 29 (Study 2) with CFR <0.2 were unaffected.
- No temperature or exercise-duration data. Supports R14 (flat face as the key variable; CFR can be taken with a soft tape) and obesity as modifiable. Does not support any heat threshold.

**#38 Fawcett A et al. Animals 2019, 9(1):3 (LINKS says 2018). Tier F-skim.** Expert-perspective review (Australian veterinarians + PetSure insurance claims). Relevant statements: extreme brachy (Bulldog, French Bulldog, Pug) 3.5x more likely to have at least one upper respiratory tract disorder; "approximately 50% of Pugs and French Bulldogs, and 45% of Bulldogs had clinically significant airway disease" after a gentle three-minute trot; owner survey of 61 Pugs and 39 French Bulldogs referred for surgery: 88% severe exercise intolerance with prolonged recovery, 56% sleep problems, 50% significant heat sensitivity; plethysmography study: 5 of 52 brachycephalic vs 0 of 53 non-brachycephalic dogs withdrawn from a heat challenge for respiratory distress; overweight brachy dogs less heat tolerant. Advice: "Patients should not be exercised on warm or humid days, should be kept in a cool area of the house in summer months", manage weight, avoid over-exertion. Cooling protocol: temperature >39.5 C actively cool; stop at 39.4 C. **No ambient-temperature (e.g., 20 C) threshold anywhere in the text.**
- Behavioural section re-cites McGreevy 2013 (short-skulled: more compulsive staring, self/allo-grooming, dog-directed aggression; less stranger fear, barking, chasing).

**#39 Tomlinson F, Liu NC, Sargan DR, Ladlow JF 2026, PLoS One 21:e0340604. Tier W (full text, second pass).** 898 dogs, 14 breeds (Pug/French Bulldog/Bulldog excluded from conformation models; their grades from a 2016 study). Respiratory functional grade (RFG) by auscultation at rest and after a 3-minute exercise test: 0 none; 1 stethoscope-only noise; 2 audible; 3 exercise intolerance/cyanosis (2-3 clinically significant). Grade-0 share: Pekingese 10.9%, Japanese Chin 17.4%, King Charles Spaniel 39.8%; moderate risk (25-50% grade 0): Griffon Bruxellois, Boston, Dogue de Bordeaux, King Charles, Shih Tzu; mild (>50%): Staffordshire Bull Terrier, CKCS, Chihuahua, Boxer, Affenpinscher, Pomeranian; Maltese inconclusive. Multivariable: overweight BCS **OR 1.8 (1.3-2.6)**, age OR 1.08 (1.01-1.16), nostril stenosis, lower CFR; sex/neuter not significant; **Tjur R2 0.2, AUC 0.75 (CFR alone ~16%)**. Within-breed BCS OR 9.7 Affenpinscher, 2.2 CKCS, 5.0 Shih Tzu. Limitations: volunteer/event recruitment, low power, RFG assumed to track BOAS beyond its three validation breeds, King Charles result unexplained. **No exercise or heat advice** (exercise used only as a diagnostic test). Supports a conformation + BCS rule (R14) and weight as the modifiable lever.

**#37b Hall EJ, Carter AJ, O'Neill DG 2020, Animals 10:1324 (PMC7459873). Tier W.** VetCompass 2016: **905,543 dogs; 1,259 heat-related illness events in 1,222 dogs; 74.2% exertional (652 events)**. Exertional-event odds ratios: brachycephalic vs mesocephalic 1.32 (1.10-1.60) (environmental OR 2.36; vehicular 3.07); bodyweight vs <10 kg: 10-<20 kg 2.52 (1.97-3.22), 20-<30 2.27, 30-<40 2.30, 40-<50 2.15, >=50 2.21; breeds vs Labrador: Chow Chow 7.00 (3.00-16.33), Bulldog 3.73, French Bulldog 2.96, Greyhound 2.11 (1.08-4.16), English Springer 1.85, CKCS 1.69, Staffordshire Bull Terrier 1.56. **Age: <2 y is the reference group; older age bands have lower exertional odds** (8-<10 y OR 0.48; >=12 y 0.54), while >=12 y has higher *environmental* odds (3.15). "The paper does not set a specific ambient threshold." It cites Lilja-Maula: Bulldogs developing hyperthermia "just standing in ambient room temperature (21 C)". No exercise guidance; main recommendation is owner education about exertion in hot weather and earlier-season campaigns (warm spells in March/April).
- **Hall EJ, Carter AJ, Chico G, Bradbury J, Gentle LK, Barfield D, O'Neill DG 2022, Vet Sci 9:231 (PMC9144152). Tier W (full text, second pass).** VetCompass: 905,543 dogs in 2016; 856 HRI events 2016-2018 (592 with a recorded trigger). **The temperature variable is the median maximum daily WBGT (wet-bulb globe temperature, a heat-stress index, not air temperature): 16.9 C overall (IQR 14.8-18.9); 16.5 C exertional; 17.8 C environmental.** Fatal outcome: brachycephalic OR 3.01 (1.60-5.67), age >=12 y OR 8.87 (2.87-27.41), severe clinical grade OR 64.92; severe disease: >=12 y OR 5.89, 10-<20 kg OR 2.71. Authors: small number of events, low R2 ("an important variable is missing"), weather-station temperatures may not match the dog's experience, historical records. The 16.5/16.9 numbers are WBGT, so not directly comparable to an air-temperature threshold like 20 C.

## 3. Weighting check: does any source support 0.25 breed / 0.75 observed?

**No.** No source in this folder (or the MacLean, Dutrow, Morrill papers that were checked) computes or recommends a prior/observation weight. What exists:
- Breed explains ~9% of individual behavioral variance on average (ges 0.034-0.253 across 78 breeds).
- 67.2 +/- 7.5% of confirmed purebreds fall within one SD and 95.4% within two SD (Morrill's wording; the reference SD was not checked in the text I read).

Derived arithmetic (my own, not in any source; assumptions: breed ges ~0.09 is the between-breed share of total variance; owner-rated trait reliability r_obs):
- Typical between-breed SD = sqrt(0.089) ~ 0.30 total SD (max sqrt(0.253) ~ 0.50). So a breed's mean is on average only ~0.3 SD from the population mean. A prior delta of **+/-1 step** on a 5-step scale is probably larger than most breed offsets (depending on the item SD); +/-0.5 is more defensible.
- Best linear estimate of a dog's true trait from a noisy owner score and its breed mean: `est = breed_mean + k * (obs - breed_mean)`, `k = within_true_var / (within_true_var + error_var)`. With within-breed true variance share 0.91*r_obs and error share (1 - r_obs): r_obs 0.7 gives breed weight ~0.32; 0.8 gives ~0.21; 0.9 gives ~0.11. So **0.25 is inside the plausible range for owner reliability ~0.75-0.8** (test-retest for CFQ items: strong for 27/33 items at 6 weeks, moderate-to-weak at 1 year) but it is a coincidence of assumptions, and the breed weight should *fall* as the number of answered items rises. Implement as `w_breed = (1 - r)/((1 - r) + 0.91 r)` with a configurable `r` (default ~0.75-0.8), clamp 0.1-0.3, flagged D.
- With **no** observed data, the sensible prior is the breed mean (already shrunk) and wide uncertainty, not "0.5 x prior + 0.5 x neutral"; with a 9% R2 the prior should be described to owners as "typical for the breed, a weak guide".

## 4. Heat threshold check (~20 C for brachycephalic dogs)

No source supports ~20 C. Sources close to it: Lilja-Maula (cited in Hall 2020): Bulldogs developed hyperthermia in a 21 C room; Hall 2022: median maximum daily WBGT 16.9 C on HRI days (heat index, not air temperature); Zgank 2023 (secondary): ~23 C and >=24.1 C in other studies; Fawcett 2019: avoid exercise on warm or humid days. So 20 C is not a validated safe line and may be too permissive for brachycephalic, heavy (>=10 kg), Chow-type and obese dogs. Recommendation: no single magic number; use tiers (`CAUTION`, `NO_EXERTION`) with conservative default constants and humidity, show-the-sources text, and mark D until reviewed by a veterinarian (RECOVER/BSAVA/AVMA-type guidance was not retrieved here).

## 7. Gaps and open questions

1. **No source quantifies how much an engine should lean on breed vs observation**; section 3 is derived arithmetic. Needs calibration with TuringCare's own intake + 4-week logs.
2. **Heat thresholds**: no vet-association source retrieved (RECOVER, BSAVA, AVMA, UK Brachycephalic Working Group). Needs a vet-reviewed table (temperature x humidity x breed/weight tier).
3. **C-BARQ licensing**: Penn terms-of-use page not retrieved; get written permission or keep original items only.
4. **DAFRS, CFQ, LCAS item wording and licensing** unclear; DAFRS items are in a supplement not provided.
5. **Dutrow Table S3** and Morrill per-breed tables are needed to build group priors from data with shrinkage; text only gives the top correlations.
6. Still abstract/metadata only after a second retrieval pass (no legal open full text found): Hsu & Serpell 2003, Duffy 2008, Wright 2011, Ley 2008/2009, Svartberg 2006, Turcsan 2011, Olson 2015, Voith 2009, Radosta 2012, HCPI 2009, Krontveit 2012. Request author copies before citing numbers from them.
7. The Mills pain prevalence figures are clinic-series; the true prevalence among pet-dog behavior complaints is unknown (earlier estimates 5-23%).
8. Age cutoff for "late onset": no numeric source; README (>=7 y / last 25% lifespan) and NOTES (5-6 y) disagree.
9. Whether owner-answered intake items are stable over weeks (CFQ 6-week test-retest strong for most items; 1-year weak) should be measured in-product.
10. Neutering: all behavioral evidence is cross-sectional with large confounding; keep out of the engine.
11. Brachycephalic **behavior** rules (McGreevy: more compulsive staring, fewer stranger-fear/chasing) are breed-level and small; they should not drive any module.
12. The Packer CFR owner measurement has no published inter-rater reliability.
