# 02 Problem behaviors, behavior-modification protocols, and when to refer

Scope: fear/anxiety, leash reactivity, dog-dog and human-directed aggression, resource guarding, separation-related problems (SRP), noise/storm sensitivity, handling/vet fear, jumping, mouthing, barking, compulsive behaviors. Written for a deterministic, no-LLM engine that never diagnoses and never prescribes.
Source numbers `[n]` refer to rows in `LINKS.md`. Confidence: high / med / low = strength of support for the rule as a rule, not for the underlying fact.

## 1. Key findings

### 1.1 Prevalence and structure of problems
- Owner-perceived problems are the norm: 31% / 35% of owners at 6 / 9 months [14, 78]; 42.1% at 12 months, led by barking, jumping up, lead pulling, recall; jumping did not decline with age [62].
- Noise sensitivity is the most common anxiety-related trait (32% of 13,700 Finnish dogs) [34]; 52% of dogs at least partly firework-fearful, onset mostly in year 1 [28]. Strongest co-occurrences: fear with aggression; hyperactivity/inattention with separation behavior and compulsion [34]. Repetitive behavior co-occurs with aggression and hyperactivity [42].
- Aggression is 72% of a US behavior-referral caseload [41]. Human-directed aggression associates with fearfulness, older age, male, small size, no dog companion, first-time owner [37]; early-life risk factors include source, early rehoming, low socialization, inexperienced owner, inconsistent management [38]. Breed alone is a poor predictor [52].
- Fear and frustration are hard to tell apart even for specialists [53]; terminology like "resource guarding" needs explicit operational definition [39]; shelter "guarding" labels did not predict guarding in the new home for >50% of dogs [40].
- Bites: ~25% of people report ever being bitten, one-third needed treatment, 55% by an unfamiliar dog [48]; children are the highest-risk group and psychological sequelae (PTSD, phobia) are documented [50, 51]. Injury-associated interactions: grooming, medicating, removing food/toys, breaking up fights, hugging/kissing [47]; clinic bites are mostly to hands (63%) [46].

### 1.2 Pain and medical causes come first
- In referral caseloads of several specialists, pain was involved in 28-82% of behavior cases (conservative ~1/3); musculoskeletal, GI and dermatologic conditions dominate; relationship categories: direct manifestation, hidden driver of secondary signs, exacerbation, adjunctive signs [1].
- Clues that pain/medical should be suspected: severity out of proportion to trigger, spread/generalization, slow or no progress under behavior modification, unexplained relapses, new onset in middle-aged/older dogs, gait/posture changes [1, 14]. Medical conditions and drugs that change behavior: Cushing's, osteoarthritis, cognitive dysfunction, steroid polyphagia, over-treated hypothyroidism [14].
- Practical sequence from referral-centre guidance: history + exam + routine bloods/urinalysis; if pain plausible, run analgesia trial for at least 4-6 weeks (ideally 8) alongside behavior modification [14]. It is not necessary to have a clean bill of health before referral [13].
- Engine implication: the engine cannot examine a dog, so it must route pain suspicion to a veterinarian and treat "vet cleared" as an input.

### 1.3 Method: reward-based, sub-threshold, no flooding
- AVSAB, AAHA, IAABC, ACVB all converge: reward-based methods only; aversive tools (prong, choke, shock) and intimidation/flooding have no role; no evidence they outperform reward-based methods [2, 3, 10, 11, 68]. Controlled studies: e-collars give no consistent efficacy benefit and more tension/yawning [55, 56]; aversive-school dogs show more stress behavior, higher cortisol and "pessimistic" bias [54]. Prong collars were not superior for pulling [58]. Caveat: most training-method evidence is observational or correlational [3, 69].
- Aversive methods "often suppress signals of impending aggression, rendering any aggressive dog more dangerous" [2]. Dominance/alpha framing is rejected [5].
- Core techniques as defined by AAHA/AVSAB [2, 3, 7]: management/avoidance first ("protection is the first treatment step"), desensitization (below the reaction threshold, gradual), counterconditioning (pair trigger with something the dog likes), alternate/incompatible behavior, environmental enrichment, medication when indicated. Flooding is "never recommended" [2].
- DS/CC evidence is mostly case series and small experiments (e.g. 100% of 91 dogs in an old series; combined DS+CC needs fewer trials than DS alone, as cited in [21]); noise-fear DS/CC with recordings is called a gold standard but owner-reported success was only 54.5% in the largest sample versus ~69% for relaxation training and similar for real-world counterconditioning [27]. Not every dog benefits from recordings [27].
- No peer-reviewed outcome studies were found for BAT, Look At That, Engage-Disengage or the Overall relaxation protocol; they are practitioner-consensus methods [63, 64, 80]. Treat as plausible, low evidence.

### 1.4 Separation-related problems
- DS/CC-style graded absences are the best-supported behavioral approach; medication is an adjunct; owner time commitment is the main failure point; avoid long absences early; no punishment; keep owner instructions to five or fewer [21]. Feuerbacher & Muir: improvement vs baseline in all 5 dogs but only 1/5 reached 5 minutes after four sessions, so progress is slow [22].
- Early-life risk/protective: aversive puppy training and "fussing" on return raise risk; >=9 h sleep and enclosed overnight space protective (correlational) [24]; another dog in the home does not prevent SRP [21]; confinement in a crate is not reliably protective [21].
- Diagnosis is by indirect evidence (destruction, neighbor reports) and is under-diagnosed [21]; self-injury and property damage are severity red flags [21].
- Sudden onset in an adult dog needs medical rule-out first (pain, CCD, GI) [1, 14].

### 1.5 Noise/storm sensitivity
- Combination of management (safe place, mask sound, no forced exposure), behavior training (ad hoc counterconditioning, relaxation, DS/CC with recordings), and where indicated medication [27]. Prevention: positive association with sounds in puppies and adults is highly effective [27, 28].
- Recovery after a firework exposure: ~3/4 by next morning, ~10% up to 1 day, ~12% up to 1 week, >3% weeks to months [28]. Practical implication for scheduling: do not run noise DS/CC in the days after a real event for dogs with slow recovery (engine heuristic, low confidence).
- Nutraceuticals, DAP, homeopathy, etc. mostly not strong enough alone; caregiver placebo effect 30-50% [27]; compression wraps: weak evidence [30]; DAP collar: lab RCT positive, field mixed [31]; one small supplement RCT positive [33]. Medication with placebo-controlled support exists (dexmedetomidine gel, trazodone, imepitoin, gabapentin, alprazolam) [27, 74]: vet-only.

### 1.6 Aggression, bite risk, and the bite scale
- AAHA: aggression, self-injury, multiple diagnoses, profound phobia, non-response, deep bites, or injury to immunocompromised persons must go to a DACVB; "under no circumstances should aggression ... be referred to a trainer for primary treatment" [2]. Trainer referral is for normal-but-unwanted behavior (jumping), unruly behavior (pulling), manners [2].
- Dogs Trust/RCVS referral triggers: no obvious medical cause; risk of injury to owner/public; complex presentation; long-term training/management needed [14]. AVSAB: fear, anxiety or aggression: vet with behavior knowledge, DACVB, or credentialed consultant [8]. ACVB: when behavior is serious enough that euthanasia is considered, the standard of care is a DACVB [11].
- Dunbar bite scale (expert-opinion prognosis) [19, 20]: L1 no contact; L2 teeth on skin no puncture; L3 1-4 shallow punctures; L4 deep puncture; L5 multiple L4 bites; L6 fatal. L1-2 are >99% of "bites" with excellent prognosis via CC/DS/handling; L3 fair-good with owner compliance and extra safety; L4 poor and "should not remain in a home with children"; L5-6 euthanasia should be considered. The scale rates injury, not intent or context; prognosis statements are not trial-based.
- Management and safety are the cornerstone [2]; interim "behavioural first aid": list contexts and triggers, avoid or minimize triggers, containment (gates, leads, muzzle), do not reinforce, rule out medical, log and video if safe [13].
- Muzzle: teach before needed; basket muzzle (allows panting, drinking, treats); two stages (nose-in, then strap); sessions 5-10 min; dog controls pace; never use a muzzle to force a procedure on a struggling dog; constricting nylon muzzles only for very brief use; history of biting or handling sensitivity means involve a professional [17, 18, 29].
- Medication for owner-directed aggression: fluoxetine arm outperformed toy/control at 4 weeks in an RCT (n=80) [43]. Used here only as a referral signal.

### 1.7 Handling and vet fear
- Fear generalizes after a single bad visit; aggression is almost always fear-driven [6]. Reward-based handling training and DS/CC are recommended; low-stress handling, minimal restraint, food/toys, order procedures from least to most unpleasant; very fearful dogs may need pre-visit medication or chemical restraint (vet decision) [29].

### 1.8 Puppy and young-dog behaviors
- Puppy mouthing, jumping, barking are normal-but-unwanted and suit trainer-level help or engine exercises [2, 62]. AVSAB: socialize during the first 3 months, class from 7-8 weeks with vaccination started [4]; evidence base for classes is thin [60, 61]. 10% of puppies fearful at 8-week exam were fearful at 18 months and "patients do not outgrow pathologic fear"; fearful puppies should not be flooded with more exposure [2].
- Compulsive/repetitive behavior: associations with low exercise, no dog companion, aggression, hyperactivity [42]; AAHA lists self-injury and ritualized behavior as referral/medical signals [2].

### 3.4 Protocol step sequences (DS/CC family)

General DS/CC template (practitioner consensus + AAHA/AVSAB definitions; the numbers below are engine defaults, tune with a DACVB/IAABC advisor):
1. Set up: choose trigger, intensity ladder (e.g. distance, volume, duration, intensity), a rich reward (food the dog loves), a low-stress location; confirm management already in place.
2. Determine the dog's threshold from owner-reported distance/intensity at which first stress signal appears; start well below (suggest >= 50% farther/quieter than first signal).
3. Present trigger at level L; reward immediately and continuously while it is present (trigger on -> treat; trigger off -> treat stops) [2, 18].
4. Observe response: relaxed (loose body, takes food, soft face) vs stress (see 3.7).
5. Advance one small step only after criterion met.
6. Regress if stress/ reactivity appears.
7. Stop session after short fixed time or on first criterion breach; end on success.

| ID | Rule | Default | Sources | Conf |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-DSCC-01 | Advance criterion | 3 consecutive trials at level L with relaxed behavior (takes food, no stress signals) across 2 sessions on different days, then change ONE parameter by a small step | practitioner consensus; [2, 21] | low-med |
| R-DSCC-02 | Regress criterion | any one of: stops taking food, freezing, lunging, barking, whale-eye, panting/pacing: go back 2 levels and end session; two regress events in a session -> stop; two regress sessions in a row -> drop one more level; three in a row -> stop and trigger R-REF-01 (pain/medical check) and, if aggression is involved, R-REF-03 | [2, 14, 1] | med |
| R-DSCC-03 | Session length | 3-5 minutes, 1-3 sessions per day for SRP/noise; 5-10 min cap for muzzle/handling | [17, 18] | low-med |
| R-DSCC-04 | Never flood; if trigger appears unexpectedly during training, create distance, abandon the session, score as regress | [2, 3] | high |
| R-DSCC-05 | Reward value: highest-value rewards only during CC; do not use the same food as everyday kibble | [27, 29] | low-med |
| R-DSCC-06 | Generalization: after criterion at L in context A, move to context B at reduced difficulty (2 levels back) | practitioner consensus | low |
| R-DSCC-07 | Recovery gap: if dog shows trigger-stacked day (multiple stressors) skip training; after a major scare (fireworks), wait at least until normal appetite and play return; for slow-recovery dogs allow up to a week | [28]; no published trigger-stacking threshold | low |

Specific modules (stages are the engine's data; each stage has pass criteria per R-DSCC-01/02):

- Protocol for Relaxation (Overall, Tier 1) [63]: prerequisite sit/down for ~15 s; ~15 task sheets, progressive duration/distraction/distance, handler moves, doorbell; pace is dog-led (weeks to months). Use as the foundation module ("settle on mat"). Confidence in efficacy: low-med; engine data should be stored as a ladder of stages (duration x distraction x location) rather than copying the text (copyright).
- Look At That / Engage-Disengage [80]: marker (click/"yes") when the dog looks at the trigger calmly, reward by handing treat at the owner's side; criterion: dog looks, then turns to owner on its own; only at sub-threshold distance; stop if dog fixates/stiffens. Low confidence in outcomes; widely used.
- BAT [64]: dog-chosen approach/retreat setups at sub-threshold distance with a trained helper, functional reward (distance); owner must have management outside setups; requires a trained instructor for safe setups; engine should present BAT only as "find a qualified instructor" guidance or as light "let the dog sniff and choose distance" tips; never for dogs with bite history.
- Noise DS/CC [27]: recordings at volume below the first reaction; graded volume; plus ad hoc counterconditioning in real events; relaxation training. If dog panics at any volume -> R-REF-06.
- Handling/vet [29, 6]: start with touch of body area the dog tolerates, pair with food; add restraint and equipment (muzzle, towel, table) in small steps; vet visit "happy visits"; pre-visit meds are vet decision; if dog bites during handling -> R-REF-02.
- Muzzle [17, 18]: 9 stages: muzzle appears -> treat; muzzle at nose -> treat; nose in -> treat; hold longer; fed through front; touch straps; clip loosely; fit properly; wear with positive activity; short sessions; basket muzzle only; no leaving unattended; never as punishment or when dog is under stress.
- Separation (graded absence) [21, 22]: Level 0 dog calm when owner moves around; level 1: step out of sight 1-5 s; build to door close; then exit/return at 5, 10, 20, 30, 60 s... doubling only after 3 calm trials; avoid long absences early; use leaving-cues desensitization separately; owners must record via video/audio; dog fails if it vocalizes/paces: go back two durations. Durations are illustrative. If dog panics/self-injures at any duration -> R-REF-06. Gate on medical check for adult-onset.
- Puppy mouthing/jumping/barking [2, 62]: teach alternative behaviors (sit to greet, hold toy), reinforce alternative; manage with leash/gate; no punishment; consider age/exercise/sleep; if mouthing hurts and dog is >5 months with fear/frustration or bite-like intensity, R-REF-02/03.

### 3.7 Stress signals (data dictionary for owner check-ins)
Use AAHA Table 1 [2] and Shepherd ladder [65] to build a controlled vocabulary used by both advance/regress rules and the safety log. Low-intensity: lip/nose licking, yawning, head turn-away, scanning, shifting weight, shaking off, paw lift, slow movement. Medium: panting without exertion, pacing, whale-eye, ear pinned back, tail low/tucked, freezing/immobility, refusing food, trembling, hypersalivation. High/danger: stiffening/staring, growling, lip curl/air snap, lunging, snap, bite, shut-down/collapse, panic escape. Rung order varies; dogs may skip rungs if warnings were previously punished [65].

## 4. Safety / referral red flags (explicit checklist)

Stop generating training exercises for the affected domain and show referral guidance if ANY is true:

**Medical / pain first (-> veterinarian)**
- [ ] Reported injury, pain, limping, stiffness, reluctance to jump/stairs/handling
- [ ] Sudden-onset behavior change (days to a few weeks), or new behavior in a dog >= 7 years
- [ ] Night waking/disorientation/new fears in an older dog (cognitive decline)
- [ ] GI/skin/appetite/thirst changes alongside behavior
- [ ] New medication or recent dose change (steroids, thyroid, etc.)
- [ ] No progress or relapse after ~4-6 weeks of a correctly applied plan (pain/medical re-check)
- [ ] Severity or generalization out of proportion to the trigger

**Aggression / bite (-> DACVB/ECAWBM, or CAB/CAAB via vet referral)**
- [ ] Any bite with skin contact, any bite to a child, any L>=3 bite, multiple bites
- [ ] Aggression toward people with escalating frequency/intensity or multiple contexts
- [ ] Dog-dog fights with injury, or aggression within the household with injury
- [ ] Redirected bite to handler
- [ ] Children, elderly or immunocompromised people at risk and any human-directed aggression
- [ ] Resource guarding that escalates to snap/bite or involves people/space/children
- [ ] Owner considering rehoming or euthanasia for behavior
- [ ] Owner has used or been told to use aversive/dominance methods in an aggressive dog

**Fear / anxiety / compulsion (-> vet then DACVB)**
- [ ] Panic, escape attempts, self-injury, or damaged teeth/claws
- [ ] Shut-down, freezing, refusing food > a day or two at home
- [ ] Noise phobia with panic, > 1 day recovery, or worsening each season
- [ ] Separation distress with injury, prolonged vocalization, elimination, or adult onset
- [ ] Compulsive/repetitive behavior that interrupts normal activity or causes self-injury
- [ ] Fear that is worsening, generalizing, or not improving in a young dog

**Safety management that must always be shown with any aggression flag**
- [ ] Supervision/separation from children; no punishment; do not force interactions; muzzle only after professional input

## 5. Gaps in `apps/api/src/lib/safety-policy.ts` vs the literature (read-only review)

What the policy does well: structured inputs only (never reads free text); conservative suppression of every exercise; injury_or_pain to veterinarian; bite risk, severe fear, severe behavior concern to veterinary behaviorist; persisted aggression/fear signals have no expiry; worsening trend gate (>= 2 high-intensity entries and >= 2 "harder" check-ins in 14 days). This aligns with AAHA "aggression never to a trainer" [2], Mills [1] and Dogs Trust referral triggers [14].

Gaps and suggestions (no code changes made):
1. **Pain/medical triggers are narrow.** Only `injury_or_pain` reported within 90 days. Literature wants pain/medical review for sudden onset, adult/senior new behavior, out-of-proportion severity, non-progress/relapse after weeks, and known conditions/meds [1, 14]. Chronic pain (e.g. osteoarthritis) should not expire after 90 days; consider keeping it active until a vet clears it, or until re-confirmed. Consider new structured signals: `sudden_onset_change`, `senior_new_behavior`, `no_progress_after_weeks`, `known_medical_condition`, `medication_change`.
2. **No bite severity gradation.** Any aggression/bite signal maps to the same output. A Dunbar level (1-6), bite to child, number of bites, and victim type would allow R-REF-02 style tiers and urgent messaging [19]. Current policy would not distinguish a level 1 lunge from a level 4 bite.
3. **No household-risk modifiers.** Children, vulnerable persons, multiple dogs, and visitors are not in the policy. AAHA and Dunbar make child presence a top modifier [2, 19, 50].
4. **Suppression is all-or-nothing.** Literature supports showing management/safety "first aid" content (barriers, no punishment, muzzle introduction after professional input, log/video) while exercises are suppressed [13, 2]. Confirm the referral screen includes that content; otherwise the owner receives referral advice without interim safety steps.
5. **Worsening trend refers to `credentialed_trainer`.** For worsening aggression or fear, literature points to veterinarian/veterinary behaviorist, not trainer-only [2, 8, 11]. Recommend: worsening intensity -> `veterinarian` (medical + pain re-check) with `veterinary_behaviorist` if aggression/fear domain, `credentialed_trainer` only for non-clinical problems (jumping, pulling).
6. **No self-injury, compulsive, panic-escape, noise-panic, or separation-injury signal types.** `severe_fear_or_panic` may cover some, but compulsive behavior and self-injury are explicit AAHA referral criteria [2].
7. **Fear in puppies.** AAHA warns fearful puppies should not receive more forced exposure and that pathological fear is not outgrown [2]; policy has no age-aware handling.
8. **Aversive history input.** Prior aversive/dominance training is a risk modifier [3, 54] but is not a policy input.
9. **Severe signals never expire**, which is conservative; there is no resolution path ("vet cleared", "DACVB cleared with plan"). Add an authoritative, auditable way to record clearance and a re-entry plan (e.g. professional-supplied constraints) [11, 14].
10. **Threshold design**: `HIGH_INTENSITY_THRESHOLD = 4` and the 2+2 rule in 14 days are plausible but have no literature basis (no published threshold). Mark as tunable; consider adding "any single high-intensity aggression event" as a trigger.
11. **Medication language**: policy has no medication field; keep that way. A referral message listing "a veterinarian can discuss medication" is consistent with the literature [3, 27].

## 6. Open questions / gaps

- No peer-reviewed outcome evidence for BAT, LAT, Engage-Disengage, or the Overall protocol in my searches; engine thresholds/step counts for DS/CC are practitioner defaults and need a DACVB/IAABC advisor to confirm.
- Trigger stacking: found only blog-level sources; no validated recovery interval. Treat as an owner-education concept, not a quantitative engine input.
- Prognosis by bite level is Dunbar's opinion; no cohort data found. Also no validated tool for owners to self-grade bite level.
- Medication evidence: key RCTs paywalled (Korpivaara 2017, Podberscek 1999, Blackwell 2006); engine does not use medication anyway.
- Not found open-access: Shepherd ladder chapter, Overall textbook, Ziv 2017/Fernandes 2017 reviews, Dinwoodie/Casey owner-aggression epidemiology; DOIs for #65, #68 need verification.
- Muzzle evidence: only practitioner/university guidance; no trials of muzzle-conditioning efficacy.
- Gaps: noise DS/CC dosing, compulsive-disorder owner-level management, adolescent-onset fear periods, behavior-focused puppy classes outcomes, owner-adherence data for engine-length programs (suggest <=5 instructions [21]).
- Position statements not retrieved: BSAVA referral page (403), ABTC/IAABC procedures pages (404), CCPDT LIMA (404), Dogs Trust first-aid owner page (gated). RCVS referral wording quoted via Dogs Trust only [14].
- Terminology differences (UK "clinical animal behaviourist", US "DACVB", EU "ECAWBM", IAABC CDBC, CAAB): engine should map credential strings to a controlled list per region.
