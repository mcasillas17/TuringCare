# Section 2. Corrections (consolidated, ordered by impact on engine rules)

## 2.1 Claims that change a rule or a rule's confidence

1. **NOTES l.37, l.65, R-NOISE-02 ("medication with placebo-controlled support: dexmedetomidine, trazodone, imepitoin, gabapentin, alprazolam [27, 74]").** Riemer 2023 describes only dexmedetomidine, imepitoin and gabapentin as placebo-controlled; trazodone was open/comparative ("no placebo group"), alprazolam rests on an owner survey ("reported effectiveness ... 90.6%"). Also [32] McPeake 2017 is an _uncontrolled_ case series that itself calls for "a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial", so it is not placebo-controlled support. Fix: list three; label the others "open/owner-reported". Row 74 numbers: 72% (64/89) good/excellent vs 37% (34/93) placebo, n=182, Orion-sponsored.
2. **LINKS 40 (McGuire) takeaway "history-based inputs, not labels, should drive rules"; NOTES l.12, R-RG-01.** Not supported: owner-reported surrender history was equally weak (PPV 38.5%, kappa 0.26). ">50% did not guard" is true for scenario Q1 only (52.6%); 66.7/76.5/90/95% did not guard in Q2-Q5. Fix: both labels and histories have low PPV (24-47%); absence of guarding is more reliable (NPV 89-98%); capture the specific resource and context.
3. **LINKS 58 (Johnson & Wynne) / R-MGT-03 / R-EQ-01 ("harness/head halter [58]").** Study is a fixed-order (non-randomized) within-subject experiment, 23 shelter dogs, one handler, not "randomized crossover-style". Head halters were NOT tested; only front-connection harness vs martingale vs prong vs flat. Head-halter support in R-MGT-03/R-EQ-01 has no source in this set (convention).
4. **NOTES l.13 / LINKS 48, 50, 51 "children are the highest-risk group [48, 50, 51]".** Westgarth 2018 [48] does not show this (3/48 surveyed children bitten; under-5s not studied). Westgarth 2024 [50] says children are "more vulnerable" but cites Tulloch 2021 that admissions rise is "driven by adult cases". Support R-REF-05 with severity/location of child injuries (face/head, peak 5-9 y, mostly known dogs at home), not "highest risk". [50] is a single-database, no-appraisal review of mostly case studies, not "scoping/systematic".
5. **NOTES l.16, l.61, LINKS 1 (Mills 2020).** Table 1 range is 23-82% (abstract says 28-82%) and is the proportion where pain was _suspected_; only 16-79% of those confirmed. "Analgesia trial 4-8 weeks [14]" is not in Mills: cases used 4, 4-6, 6 weeks. "New onset in middle-aged/older dogs [1]" (NOTES l.17) is not in Mills (pain aggression often presents in younger dogs). The age-7 / last-25%-of-lifespan REFER_VET trigger (README, R-REF-01, R-OLD-01) therefore has no source in [1]; it is a convention.
6. **NOTES l.25, l.29, l.142 (Sargisson 2014 [21]).** "100% of 91 dogs" was Rogerson's _fears and phobias_ series, not SRP; "combined DS+CC needs fewer trials" is a _rat_ study. The only SRP DS data are n=8 (Butler, row 73: p=0.008 vs baseline, owner consistency did not affect outcome). The graded-absence numbers (5/10/20/30/60 s, doubling after 3 calm trials, <18 months, go back two durations) are NOT in Sargisson ("very short times ... gradually increase ... if worse, reduce"). "Self-injury and property damage are severity red flags [21]" is the engine's inference. "Enclosed overnight space protective" belongs to [24], not [21].
7. **NOTES l.44, R-DSCC-03, R-VET-01, muzzle module ([17, 18, 29]).** Toronto protocol (18) specifies no session minutes ("frequent but short"), does not say "basket", and has no "never leave unattended"; its step 9 is "end on a positive note". Riemer 2021 [29] never uses "basket muzzle", "two stages", "5-10 min", or "dog controls pace": it only says soft nylon muzzles that restrict panting are "only suitable for very short-term use, such as during an injection" and muzzles must not be used to "manhandle a resisting dog". "5-10 min" and "basket" must rest on Cornell [17] (not in my set; verify there) or be marked convention.
8. **NOTES l.48, R-VET-01 ("aggression is almost always fear-driven [6]; fear generalizes after a single bad visit").** Riemer 2021: aggression in the vet setting "ensues most frequently due to fear, pain or fear of pain"; "every negative experience may increase fear during subsequent veterinary visits". Not "almost always"; "generalizes" is not stated. (AVSAB 2024 [6] is the other half's source; check there.)
9. **NOTES l.35 "Prevention (positive association) is highly effective [27, 28]".** Riemer 2023 says "appears to be highly effective" resting on one correlational survey (Riemer 2019: median fear 1 vs 4) and a puppy study with "no long-term effects". Soften: "strongly associated; causality not shown".
10. **NOTES l.10/36 [28].** Citation is PLoS ONE 14(**9**):e0218150 (LINKS says 14(7)). "Onset mostly in year 1": 45% under 1 y (median 1 y). 52% = "at least partly" affected in a self-selected survey. Recovery percentages are among fearful dogs only (next morning ~74% cumulative; <=1 wk 10.4%+1.8%; weeks 2.3%+months 1.2%). R-DSCC-07 recovery gap rests on this only.
11. **LINKS 43 / NOTES l.45 (Supanta 2026).** Partially assessor-blinded randomized trial, 80 enrolled / 57 analysed (not n=80), treatment >=4 wk; the control arm also improved significantly (-0.20), toy vs control n.s., all arms got behaviour guidance. Downgrade MED-LOW to LOW. Management "predictors" (head petting, travelling, artificial bones, living area; R2 0.34) are not causal.
12. **LINKS 54 / NOTES l.22 (Vieira de Castro 2020).** Quasi-experimental non-randomized; n=92 Phase 1, cortisol n=31, cognitive bias n=73. Cortisol difference only Aversive vs Reward (Mixed n.s.); "pessimistic" = slower latencies to all locations (no group x location interaction). Dogs with fear/aggression problems were excluded. Full cite: PLoS ONE 15(12):e0225023.
13. **LINKS 31 / NOTES l.37 (Landsberg 2015).** Alternate-ranked (non-random) allocation, n=24 of 27, Ceva-funded, only active and global fear scores significant (passive n.s.), no field data, so "field mixed" must be sourced to Riemer 2023 not [31]. Vet Rec 177(10):260.
14. **LINKS 30 / NOTES l.37 (Mathis 2024).** "Harmless adjunct" unsupported (no adverse effects _reported_; not studied). Review confidence "low"; not pre-registered; 3 of 4 studies were lab with recorded noise. Riemer 2023 reads it as "a small beneficial effect is possible", so "weak" is fair but "harmless" is not.
15. **LINKS 33 (Ramos 2024).** Industry-funded (Biolab Sanus, maker of the supplement); placebo group got the same behaviour advice; owner global-improvement, satisfaction ratings did NOT differ (e.g. p=0.261); beat placebo on LSSS and cortisol only.
16. **LINKS 23 (Karagiannis 2015).** Not "uncontrolled": 5 treated + 7 healthy untreated controls, but no placebo/randomization/blinding; authors say drug vs plan cannot be separated. Vet Res 11:80. Takeaway "affect change not only suppression" is overstated.
17. **NOTES l.52, R-REF-07 (Sulkama 2022).** Authors deliberately say "repetitive", not "compulsive" ("we do not know if the behaviour is abnormally repeated"); n=4436 analysed (dichotomised), same Finnish dataset as [34], first-time owner OR 1.58 also associated.
18. **LINKS 26, 39, 45, 60-62, 46, 47, 53, 34, 36, 37.** See per-source CORRECTIONS: Hunter (n=63; stated opinions; 5 referrals actually made; Australian legal frame); Jacobs (two-stage survey + discussion board n=36/29/14, not Delphi; authors _declined_ an ethogram; excludes guarding of people/places); Iarussi (training associated with regulatory awareness/reporting OR 4.82/1.98, not prevention; 81% refer to a behaviour-trained vet, 10% to trainers; Italian law); Howell (narrative; correlational; window 3-12 (max 14) wk; early thunder exposure <4 mo linked to later noise fear); McEvoy (single DB, ~half experimental but mostly 1950s-60s lab work; PMC-flagged correction Animals 2023;13(1):81); Kinsman (n=684-1718 per survey, 1219 at 12 mo; jumping n.s. with low power; 31/35% are Lord's subset, this paper 29.9/34.0%); Malkani (dog n conflicts between abstract/Table 1; 63% = 384 staff-reported bites with a location; UK and Ireland); Wongtawan (most-reported *triggers* among injured owners, not risk factors; internal inconsistencies 20.6% vs "~50%"); Wilson (Front Psychol 2026;17:1888439; "hard even for specialists" is the authors' premise citing their 2024 study, no empirical data); Salonen (Sci Rep 10:2962; n=13,715; 32% = "highly fearful" of >=1 noise; most common comorbidities were fear + noise sensitivity); Tiira (maternal care n.s. in solely-fearful comparison; exercise association gone for SA-only; correlational); Mikkola (dichotomised 1791 vs 7479; age effect weak p=0.032; sterilisation, weaning age, exercise, time alone not associated).
19. **NOTES l.43, R-REF-02/3.6 vs README Mode table (Dunbar [19]).** Internal contradiction, partly source-driven: Dunbar L2 = "No Puncture" (teeth contact allowed), yet NOTES R-REF-02 says "any bite with skin contact (Dunbar L>=2)" -> REFER_DACVB, 3.6 says L2 = professional help "if repeated", and README puts L1-L2 single warning in CAUTION while REFER_BEHAVIORIST includes "any bite with skin contact". Pick one definition. Also ">99% of bites are L1-L2" in the handout derives from hospital ISS data (92.4% ISS 0, 7.5% ISS 1, <1% ISS>=2), i.e. injury severity of treated incidents, not an epidemiological bite-level distribution; "1-4 shallow punctures" does not appear in the handout text (only "Shallow Puncture", "Multiple Bites"). Prognosis wording is Dunbar's opinion ("Wonderful", "fair to good if owner compliance", "poor", "dire").
20. **NOTES l.22 (IAABC [10]).** IAABC does not say "aversive tools have no role": LIMA permits Level 5 (negative punishment, negative reinforcement, extinction), calls positive punishment "rarely necessary", and says LIMA "does not justify" e-collar/choke/prong "in lieu of other effective positive reinforcement interventions". Intrusiveness may occasionally require a more intrusive effective choice. Health/medical is Level 1 of the hierarchy (supports R-REF-01). LINKS 10 "2020" has no date in the PDF (URL timestamp Oct 2020); the PDF also contains Friedman's essay and is 9 pages.

# Section 4. Gaps and open questions

- No study in the set supports **any** DS/CC step size, trial count, session length, advance/regress cutoff or recovery gap. These are engine conventions; mark as tunable constants and calibrate from logs; get DACVB/IAABC sign-off.
- SRP evidence base: n=8 (Butler), n=5 (Feuerbacher), controlled owner-reported trial (Blackwell 56%+25%) - no data on optimal absence schedule; Feuerbacher shows slow progress (1/5 at 5 min after 4 sessions).
- Head-halter support (R-MGT-03/R-EQ-01) has no source here; need a source or a "convention" flag.
- Muzzle: duration (5-10 min) and "basket" only from Cornell [17] (not verified by me) - recheck; no efficacy trials.
- Bite-level boundary (L2 vs REFER) must be decided; Dunbar prognosis is expert opinion and no validated owner self-grading exists.
- Pain screen: Mills gives categories but no validated trigger thresholds (age 7, 4 wk, 4-6 wk). HCPI/CBPI claim for row 79 unverified.
- Child-risk ranking evidence (rows 48, 50, 51) is weak/indirect; use injury severity and home-location data instead.
- Rows 63, 64, 65, 80 are books/chapters: no outcome data; rows 66, 67 need full text before any takeaway beyond definitions.
- Evidence from Finnish survey group (34, 35, 37, 42) is one dataset analysed repeatedly, not independent replications.
- Industry funding in rows 31, 32 (McPeake), 33, 74 should be flagged in the data layer.

# Section 1. Per-source digest

### Howell TJ, King T, Bennett PC. Puppy parties and beyond: the role of early age socialization practices on adult dog behavior. Vet Med (Auckl). 2015 Apr 29;6:143-153. doi:10.2147/VMRR.S62081 (PMCID PMC6067676) (read in full; references skimmed)

- Design/population/n/methods: Narrative review (no search protocol, no n, no inclusion criteria stated), La Trobe Univ. Covers early development periods (primary 0-3 wk, socialization ~3-12 wk and "certainly no later than 14 wk", juvenile to maturity), socialization effects, puppy classes, roles of breeder/owner/vet. Mostly epidemiological/correlational studies; few controlled trials.
- Key quantitative results: Few numbers. Freedman 1961: puppies without human contact before 14 wk never formed normal human bonds. Goddard & Beilharz 1986: fearfulness at 8 wk predicted adult fearfulness. Thunder exposure before 4 months was associated with later noise fear (Blackwell 2013), i.e. uncontrolled exposure can backfire. Class studies mixed: Duxbury 2003 (classes + handling -> less relinquishment, confounded by owner sex, sleeping location, etc.), Casey 2014 (classes -> less aggression to strangers, many co-factors), Ward 2003 (classes -> fewer problems), Seksel 1999 (extra handling/novel stimuli no significant gain), Batt 2008 (extra classes no effect on guide-dog success). Only RCT cited: Denenberg & Landsberg 2008, DAP collar in 8-wk class (less fear, more positive play; owner report at 12 mo). ~75% owners see vets as valuable info source; >half say best source.
- Limitations/biases: Narrative, no systematic search; relies on correlational studies and unpublished thesis (Ward 2003), books; confounding acknowledged ("some debate as to whether they can truly determine causal relationships"); no data on minimum/maximum socialization dose; sex/breed effects unstudied; ethics limits deprivation studies; Australian-centric.
- Supports/contradicts engine rules: Supports NOTES line 51 / evidence table line 71 (early socialization "moderate", classes "weak", [60]); supports R-TRI-01 (puppy exercises engine-eligible) and R-REF-08 (fearful puppy: don't push exposure) in spirit: "ensuring that the puppy enjoys these experiences and is not overly frightened"; "important not to stress puppies too much". Supports vet-first for behavior concerns (also says vets often lack behavior training, suggests referral to qualified trainers/veterinary behaviourists). Does not address 7-8 wk class start/vaccination (that is AVSAB [4]).
- CORRECTIONS:
  - LINKS row 60 takeaway "Socialization lowers adult fear/aggression" is stronger than the evidence base: text says "strong evidence of a link" but nearly all of it is correlational/epidemiological; add "association, not proven causal". Suggest: "Early socialization is associated with less adult fear/aggression (mostly correlational); evidence for puppy classes specifically mixed/unclear."
  - LINKS row 60 evidence strength "MED-LOW" is defensible; "Narrative review" correct. Citation correct (Howell, King, Bennett; Vet Med (Auckl) 2015;6:143-153; DOI matches). Add volume/pages.
  - NOTES line 51: the "10% of puppies fearful at 8 weeks still fearful at 18 months" is not in Howell (it is attributed [2]); fine, but Howell only says 8-wk fearfulness "predicted" adult fearfulness (Goddard 1986), no 10% figure.
  - NOTES line 71 "[60, 61]" fine. Missing nuance worth adding: Howell notes poorly timed/controlled exposure (thunder <4 mo) can increase later fear, and the age window is 3-12 (max 14) wk, not "first 3 months" exactly.

### Hunter T, van Rooy D, McArthur M, Bennett S, Tuke J, Hazel S. Mental Health Disease or Preventable Problem? Australian Dog Trainers' Opinions about Canine Separation Anxiety Differ with Training Style. Animals 2020;10(8):1393. doi:10.3390/ani10081393 (PMCID PMC7459922) (read in full; appendix questionnaire skimmed)

- Design/population/n/methods: Cross-sectional online survey (Jun-Nov 2016), 31 Qs, non-validated, piloted with trainers. Recruited via training organisations + snowballing. 141 submitted; 7 vets excluded; 71 incomplete excluded; n=63 analysed (41 reward-based, 22 "balanced"; 38 vs 22 for section D). 61/63 female. Fisher's exact tests, FDR-adjusted p; no multivariable model (too few). Self-defined style groups; no aversive/dominance trainers responded.
- Key quantitative results: Only 7.9% (5/63, all reward-based) referred to a vet for diagnosis; 82.5% used >=3 methods (owner reports 85-91%, video 55-68%).
