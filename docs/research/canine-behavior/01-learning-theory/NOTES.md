# 01 Learning theory and positive-reinforcement mechanics

Provenance: PDF text was read locally for AVSAB 2021, Casey 2021, Hiby 2004, Kutsumi 2013, AAHA 2015 (grep), China 2020 (partial) and PMC text for Vieira de Castro 2020. Kis 2017, Lazaro 2023 and Thrailkill 2016 were read via fetch (not saved). Many other papers are paywalled; those were summarized from abstracts or secondary reports and are flagged in LINKS.md. Rules tagged `convention` come from trainer practice, not trials, and are capped at low/med confidence.

## 1. Key findings

**Reward-based vs aversive (welfare and efficacy)**

- Survey: more punishment goes with more reported problem behaviour and no obedience gain (Hiby 2004, n=364); confrontational methods provoked aggressive responses in a large minority of clinic dogs (Herron 2009; exact % varies by summary).
- Quasi-experimental: dogs in schools using 76-84% aversive techniques showed far more stress behaviours (lip licking 55.9 vs 4.1), higher post-training cortisol, and more pessimistic judgement bias than reward-school dogs; mixed schools had elevated stress behaviours but cortisol/bias did not significantly differ from reward (Vieira de Castro 2020, n=92). Owners using >=2 aversive techniques had more pessimistic dogs (Casey 2021, matched pairs).
- Efficacy: a reward-focused programme matched or beat e-collar training for recall/sit (China 2020), criticized for non-comparable groups (Sargisson 2021). No study found showing aversives outperform reward.
- Reviews/bodies (Ziv 2017; Fernandes 2017; AVSAB 2021; AAHA 2015; ESVCE via Masson 2018) converge: use reward-based methods; aversives unnecessary. AVSAB 2021 lists as avoid: pain tools, intimidation (shouting, staring, alpha rolls), leash jerks, flooding; learner must be able to opt out.
- Caveat: nearly all data are correlational or non-randomized; owners of problem dogs may choose harsher methods.

**Reinforcers**

- Food is a stronger reinforcer than social interaction for most dogs (Feuerbacher & Wynne 2012, 2014; Lazaro 2023: 9/10 dogs worked harder for food than leisure items). Petting > vocal praise (Feuerbacher 2015). Individual variation is large; paired-stimulus preference assessment predicts reinforcer efficacy (Vicars 2014; Lazaro 2023). No data on preference stability over time.
- Reward quality drop causes negative contrast: dogs downshifted from liver to pellets refused food and disengaged (Bentosela 2009).
- High rates of food delivery reduce unwanted in-kennel behaviour (Protopopova 2015).

**Marker/clicker**

- Controlled studies show little or no advantage of a clicker over food alone or a spoken marker for simple behaviours with novices (Chiandetti 2016; Feng 2018); Smith & Davis 2008 found only more extinction resistance. Trainers use markers mainly for acquisition and often drop them later; poor conditioning of the signal in studies may mask benefits (Feng 2017).

**Schedules / extinction**

- Partial reinforcement and more unreinforced trials in training raise persistence in extinction in animal models; extinction tracks the number of unreinforced attempts (Thrailkill 2016, rats; dog data lacking). Frustration behaviour appears when reward is withheld (Jakovcevic 2013, title only). Dog-specific extinction-burst data are sparse.

**Session spacing and length**

- Meyer & Ladewig 2008 (18 beagles): once/week training needed fewer sessions (~6.7) to criterion than 5x/week (~9.0); daily dogs finished in fewer calendar days. Demant 2011 (44 beagles): once/twice weekly beat daily for acquisition; one session/day beat three consecutive sessions; retention >=4 weeks regardless. Lab beagles, simple tasks.
- Kis 2017: sleep or walk after learning were followed by improved retest; learning an unrelated task in the next hour showed no gain. Modest sample.

**Puppy classes**

- 1 h/week x 6 weeks classes at ~4 months improved command and stranger response (Kutsumi 2013, quasi-experimental). AVSAB 2008: classes from 7-8 weeks after first vaccine set (>=7 days earlier) and deworming.

**Hierarchy**

- Friedman Humane Hierarchy / LIMA (CCPDT, IAABC): health -> antecedents/environment -> positive reinforcement -> only later punishment. AVSAB 2021 goes further (no aversives). The engine should sit at the AVSAB end.

## 2. Evidence strength

| Claim                                                                         | Strength                                                             |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Reward-based at least as effective as aversive; aversives not needed          | strong (convergent, non-RCT)                                         |
| Aversives associated with stress and pessimism                                | moderate-strong (several designs incl. physiology)                   |
| Food > petting > praise; per-dog variation                                    | moderate-strong                                                      |
| Spaced > massed for acquisition                                               | moderate (two beagle studies, one lab)                               |
| Clicker superior to food alone                                                | weak/contested (nulls)                                               |
| Partial reinforcement boosts persistence                                      | moderate (basic science), weak in dogs                               |
| 80% criterion, 3 Ds, one-variable-at-a-time, session minutes, lure-fade steps | convention, no direct dog trials found                               |
| Shaping vs luring vs capturing superiority                                    | no controlled evidence found                                         |
| Extinction burst size/duration in pet dogs                                    | gap                                                                  |
| Mixed methods are harmless                                                    | contested (stress behaviours up; cortisol not significant, VdC 2020) |

## 3. ENGINE RULES

Format: ID | condition -> output | source | confidence.

**A. Method policy**

- R1 | any dog/goal -> only {positive reinforcement, negative punishment (removal of attention/access, sparing), differential reinforcement (DRI/DRA/DRO), counter-conditioning, management, environment change}. Never emit prong/choke/shock/spray/alpha-roll/leash-pop/flooding/shouting. | AVSAB 2021; AAHA 2015; VdC 2020; Casey 2021 | high
- R2 | every plan includes a learner opt-out: dog may leave at any time; end session if dog disengages twice. | AVSAB 2021 | med
- R3 | history of aggression/bite, severe fear, panic, separation anxiety, self-injury -> no training-only program; emit management + referral flag (section 4); allow only low-risk foundations. | AVSAB 2021; AAHA 2015 | high
- R4 | past aversive tool use or stress around equipment/cue -> add "rebuild trust" module (relaxation, choice, food-for-presence), lower initial criteria, new cues for old behaviours. | AVSAB 2021; VdC 2020 | med

**B. Reinforcers**

- R5 | default primary reinforcer = small soft food; play/petting as secondary or for high-play dogs; never rely on verbal praise alone. | Feuerbacher & Wynne 2012/2014/2015; Lazaro 2023 | high
- R6 | per dog build a ranked list (6-8 items; owner-run paired choices, 2-3 trials per pair in-app; lab used 15); store top 3 foods + top 2 toys; re-run ~every 4 weeks or when engagement drops. | Vicars 2014; Lazaro 2023 | med (interval low)
- R7 | tier reinforcers: T1 kibble for easy/low-distraction; T2 mid for new skills; T3 best treat/play for high distraction/recall. Distraction 1-2 -> T1/T2, 3 -> T2, 4-5 -> T3. Never downshift tier within a session. | Bentosela 2009; convention | med
- R8 | treats pea-sized (scale to dog) and training calories <= ~10% of daily intake (subtract from meals); health flags (obesity, diabetes, pancreatitis, allergy, GI) -> use part of ration or vet-approved items. | convention | med
- R9 | food refusal in session -> shorten/end, lower arousal; flag if >=3 consecutive sessions refuse food. | convention | med

**C. Marker and timing**

- R10 | marker optional: verbal "yes" by default; clicker as owner option. Condition marker with 10-20 pairings before use; food follows within ~1-3 s. | Feng 2017; Chiandetti 2016; Feng 2018 | med
- R11 | owner timing: reward within ~1-2 s of the behaviour, at the position of the criterion. | convention | med

**D. Schedule**

- R12 | acquisition (new behaviour or new criterion): continuous reinforcement (FR1), roughly 6-12 rewards/min of active teaching. | convention; Protopopova 2015 | med
- R13 | thin only after R19 met: FR1 -> ~FR2 -> VR 2-3 -> VR 3-5; step <= ~25%; if success <80% in two trials step back one level. Safety-critical behaviours (recall, leave-it) keep high-value rewards on a variable schedule for life. | convention; Thrailkill 2016 | low-med
- R14 | do not use extinction alone for self-rewarding or dangerous behaviours; pair with DRA and management. Warn owner of possible transient increase (extinction burst); if burst includes aggression/self-injury, stop and refer. | convention; AAHA 2015 | med (duration unstudied)

**E. Session structure**

- R15 | default 1-3 sessions/day, each 3-5 min (puppy <16 wk, senior, fearful: 2-3 min; experienced adults up to 5-10 min); never 3 consecutive sessions on the same skill; >=1 h gap. | Demant 2011; Meyer 2008 (frequency); minutes are convention | frequency med, minutes low-med
- R16 | owner budget T min/day: sessions = ceil(T/5), cap 3/skill/day, 2-3 skills/day; total <=15-20 min/day for puppies. T<5 -> one micro-session plus "reward calm in daily routine" tasks. 5-6 days/week with 1-2 rest days (daily OK if short). | Meyer 2008; Demant 2011; convention | med
- R17 | ~10-20 rewarded reps per session, end on a success; stop early if stress/disengagement or success <60% over last 5 trials. | convention; Chiandetti 2016 (8/10 criterion) | low-med
- R18 | after a session offer sleep/walk/play for ~1 h before a different new skill. | Kis 2017 | low-med

**F. Criteria and progression**

- R19 | advance when success >=80% over last 10 trials (8/10) in 2 consecutive sessions; hold at 60-80% and make slightly easier or raise reward rate; regress one step if <=60% for two sessions. | convention; Chiandetti 2016 | med
- R20 | 3 Ds: change one at a time; when raising one, reset others to baseline; order duration -> distance -> distraction; distraction graded 1-5 (empty room, home with family, yard, quiet street, park/other dogs). Example ladders: stay 1,3,5,10,20,30,60 s; distance 1,3,6,10 steps, out of sight. Increments ~20-50% (smaller for fearful dogs). | AKC/trainer sources | low-med
- R21 | two consecutive regressions at a step -> halve the step, or change environment/reinforcer first. | convention | med
- R22 | generalization: tag skill "reliable" only after success in >=3 locations x >=2 handlers x >=2 times of day; restart at easy criteria in each new context. | convention; weak peer evidence | med
- R23 | fluent = >=90% in 3 consecutive sessions, latency <=3 s, >=2 contexts, on VR schedule; only then fade cues or chain. | convention | low

**G. Teaching method**

- R24 | lure for simple positions (fade within 5-10 reps: food in hand follows -> same motion empty, reward from other hand -> shrink motion to cue -> add verbal cue once 8/10); capture for behaviours already offered; shaping for novel/complex behaviours (raise criterion after 3-5 successes). Cue transfer: add new cue just before the prompt, drop the prompt after >=5 successful pairings. No comparative trials. | AVSAB 2021 (lists lure/capture/shape); convention | low-med

**H. Safety/age gates**

- R25 | puppies: classes/socialization from 7-8 weeks only after >=1 vaccine set >=7 days earlier and healthy; avoid dog parks with unknown vaccination status. | AVSAB 2008 | high
- R26 | joint pain, brachycephalic, cardiac, post-surgery, senior cognitive decline -> drop physically demanding behaviours, shorten sessions, require health-clearance prompt. | convention; AAHA 2015 | med
- R27 | no outdoor training on hot surfaces or in high heat. | convention | low

## 4. Safety and referral red flags (stop plan; vet first, then DACVB/CAAB/IAABC-AAB or qualified force-free consultant)

- Growling, snapping, biting people/animals (esp. children); resource guarding with escalation; any bite breaking skin.
- Fear/panic: shaking, freezing, flight, destructive escape, drooling in confinement, self-injury.
- Sudden behaviour change at any age (pain, endocrine, neurologic, cognitive dysfunction); food refusal >24-48 h.
- Compulsive/repetitive behaviour (tail chasing, flank sucking).
- Separation anxiety signs.
- Owner uses or used shock/prong/choke/physical corrections and dog is fearful of owner/handling.
- Escalation during extinction.
- Any step needing restraint or force: remove from plan.
  (AVSAB 2021: treat aggression humanely, refer if stuck; AAHA 2015: punishment not for aggression, rule out medical causes.)

## 5. Open questions / gaps

- No dog trials found for the 80% criterion, 3 Ds ordering, pet-dog session minutes, thinning rates, lure-fade or cue-transfer protocols; R12-R24 are mostly convention. Consider platform A/B telemetry.
